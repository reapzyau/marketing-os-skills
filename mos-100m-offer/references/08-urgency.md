# Chapter 8 — Urgency

## Hormozi's why
Urgency is a function of TIME, not quantity (that's scarcity, Ch 7). They're often used together but they're different mechanisms. A defined deadline creates the urgency to act now rather than later.

## Framework — four types of urgency (all ethical)

### Type 1: Cohort-based rolling urgency
- Start clients on a regular schedule (weekly, monthly). Less frequent intakes = more urgency around each one.
- "If you sign up today, you get in our next group starting Monday. Otherwise wait until next Monday."
- **Operational bonus:** rolling cohorts let you choreograph onboarding once per week instead of every day — better experience, lower fulfillment cost.
- For small businesses: weekly cohorts are usually the sweet spot.

### Type 2: Rolling seasonal urgency
- Countdown to a real date tied to a season or holiday.
- "Monthly flavored promotions" — same core offer wrapped in a new seasonal name.
- Plan the year out, rotate them. Spike at announce + spike at expire.
- Examples: New Year Promotion (ends Jan 30), Sexy By Spring (ends Mar 31), Summer Body Sprint, Back-to-School Tune-Up, Holiday Hangover.
- **#1 strategy for local businesses** — they need more frequent novelty than national advertisers.

### Type 3: Pricing or bonus-based urgency
- Rotate discounts or bonuses on a defined cadence.
- "Let's get you started today to take advantage of the discount — we change these every 4 weeks."
- If raising prices: "The price is going up Friday. Get in now." Pulls fence-sitters forward.

### Type 4: Exploding opportunity
- The opportunity itself decays over time.
- B2B arbitrage: market corrects, so early movers win.
- Job offers that worsen each day they're unsigned.
- The offer is genuinely worth less the longer they wait — educate the prospect on the decay curve.

## Decision tree — which type fits the business?
- **Service business with regular intakes** → Type 1 (rolling cohort)
- **Local business** → Type 2 (rolling seasonal) — primary strategy
- **Long sales cycles / sales team needs ammunition** → Type 3 (price/bonus rotation)
- **Genuine time-decaying opportunity** → Type 4 (exploding) — rare, powerful when honest

## Diagnostic questions (ask the user)
Use `AskUserQuestion` for the type, then conversation for the details.

**Question:** "Which urgency type fits your business?"
Options:
- Rolling cohort — service business with regular intakes
- Rolling seasonal — local business / need novelty cadence
- Pricing or bonus rotation — sales team needs ammunition
- Exploding opportunity — the offer genuinely decays

Follow-ups:
- "What's the deadline cadence — weekly / monthly / quarterly / one-shot?"
- "What's the specific message / script for communicating the deadline?"
- "What's queued for the *next* urgency cycle (so you don't have a gap)?"

## Validation criteria (what "good" looks like)
- ONE urgency type chosen, matched to business model
- A specific deadline cadence (not "we'll figure it out")
- The next urgency campaign is already planned (no gaps)
- The message is REAL — the deadline actually means something

Push back if:
- The deadline is fake ("ends Friday" but renews automatically) — this kills trust like fake scarcity
- No follow-up urgency planned — guaranteed sales gap after this one expires
- Urgency type doesn't match business — e.g., a one-time digital course choosing "rolling cohort" when there's no cohort

## Pitfalls (Hormozi's warnings)
- **Fake deadlines.** Same as fake scarcity — short-term win, long-term death.
- **Single-shot urgency.** If you don't have the next one planned, sales fall off a cliff after this one. Plan the year.
- **Choosing urgency that doesn't match the business.** A solo coach with no cohort can't use "rolling cohort" urgency credibly. Match the type to the model.
- **Stacking too much urgency.** "Limited time + limited bonus + price going up + cohort closing" rings false. Pick one primary lever; let it breathe.
