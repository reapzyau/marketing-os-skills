# Chapter 11 — M-A-G-I-C Naming

## Hormozi's why
Same offer, new wrapper. Rotating the name keeps the offer fresh against ad fatigue and avatar shifts — without touching the underlying mechanics. Naming is **the last move**, not the first: name after the core, enhancers and guarantee are locked.

## Framework — M-A-G-I-C

Not all five components are mandatory. Typically use **2–3 of them**. Balance brevity with specificity. Test, iterate.

### M — Magnetic "Reason Why"
Start with a word/phrase that gives prospects a REASON the offer exists:
- Free, 88% Off, Giveaway
- Grand Opening, New Management, Anniversary
- Back to School, New Year, Halloween, Summer
- "Why are they making this great offer?" — answer it.

### A — Avatar
Call out the specific avatar — who it's FOR (and implicitly who it's NOT for):
- Local businesses: hyper-local — Towson MD beats Baltimore. Hinsdale IL beats Chicago.
- B2B: vertical-specific — "Salon Owners", "Retired Athletes", "Brooklyn Busy Executives"
- The implicit-egotism effect: people are drawn to things and people that resemble them.

### G — Goal
The prospect's dream outcome — 1–3 words:
- Pain Free, Celebrity Smile, 1st Place
- Never Out Of Breath, Perfect Product, Grand Slam Offer
- Little Black Dress, Double Your Profit, First Client
- High Ticket, 7 Figure, 100k

### I — Indicate a Time Interval
How long it takes to get the result. Test different framings:
- "42 days" can outperform "6 weeks" (specificity)
- 15 Minutes / 4 Hours / 21 Days / 6 Weeks / 3 Months
- Some platforms restrict timeline claims — obey their rules.

### C — Complete with a Container Word
Container words signal "this is a bundle, not a commodity." Choose from:
**Programs / cohorts:** Challenge, Blueprint, Bootcamp, Intensive, Incubator, Masterclass, Program, Detox, Experience, Summit, Accelerator, Workshop, Mastermind
**Sprints / tactical:** Fast Track, Shortcut, Sprint, Launch, Slingshot, Catapult, Liftoff
**Transformations:** Transformation, Comeback, Rebirth, Reset, Solution
**Edgy / playful:** Explosion, Hack, Cheatcode, Attack, Assault, Getaway, Meetup, Deep Dive, Game Plan

## Advanced — rhyme & alliteration (use sparingly)
**Rhymes:** Six-Pack Fast Track · Marriage Thrive Deep Dive · 12-Week 2-Putt Shortcut · Celebrity Butt Shortcut
**Alliteration:** Make Money Masterclass · Big Booty Bootcamp · Debt Detox · Real Estate Reset · Life Coach Liftoff

Don't force either. Forced rhymes are worse than no rhymes.

## When the offer fatigues — change order
1. Creative (images/video)
2. Body copy
3. Headline / wrapper (M-A-G-I-C name)
4. Duration of the offer
5. Enhancer (free / discount component)
6. Monetization structure ($100M Money Models)

Change wrapper before changing the mechanics.

## Diagnostic questions (ask the user)
Brainstorm-heavy:
1. "Walk me through the offer as it stands. What's the dream outcome in 1–3 words? What's the duration?"
2. "Brainstorm 10+ name candidates using 2–3 M-A-G-I-C components each. We'll filter after."
3. "Which 5 are strongest? Test reading them out loud — do they roll?"
4. "Pick the primary name and 2–3 backup wrappers for rotation."

## Validation criteria (what "good" looks like)
- ≥10 candidate names generated using the formula
- Primary name uses ≥2 M-A-G-I-C components
- At least one name is hyper-local / vertical-specific (if the offer fits)
- The container word feels right for the offer category (not "Masterclass" for a 1-on-1 service)
- 3+ backup names ready for the rotation cycle

Push back if:
- The name is just the dream outcome ("More Customers System") — needs avatar/interval/reason-why
- All names use the same container word — vary the rotation
- Forced rhymes or contrived alliteration — clean and clear beats clever-and-confusing
- Local business name doesn't specify the local area — go hyper-local

## Pitfalls (Hormozi's warnings)
- **Naming too early.** Naming is the last step, not the first. Premature names lock in the wrong frame.
- **Generic container words.** "Program" / "Course" / "Service" don't differentiate. Use the spicier list.
- **Forcing all 5 components.** 2–3 is usually right. Five components creates a clunky name.
- **No rotation plan.** A single name will fatigue. Have backups ready.
- **Naming for local without local specificity.** "Fitness Challenge" loses to "Towson Moms Fitness Challenge".

## Workbook output expected
A primary name + 3+ backup wrappers, each using ≥2 M-A-G-I-C components, with a rotation plan for when fatigue sets in.
