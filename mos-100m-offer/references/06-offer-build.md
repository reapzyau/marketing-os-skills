# Chapter 6 — The 5-Step Offer Build (The Heart)

## Hormozi's why
This is where the core offer is actually built. Six exercises, done in strict order — skip none, don't reorder. The output is the *core* offer (pre-enhancement). Scarcity/urgency/bonuses/guarantees/naming come AFTER this, not before.

## Framework — the 5 steps (do in order)

### Step 1 — Identify the Dream Outcome
One sentence. Specific. Emotional. The ultimate desire of the avatar.

NOT: "Get more customers."
YES: "Have a fully-booked schedule of 30 new gym members within 30 days, without dropping your tools or learning ads yourself."

The dream outcome should already exist in the avatar work (from Ch 2). Pull it forward. Refine if necessary.

### Step 2 — List Every Problem
Brainstorm. No filtering. Aim for **15–30 problems** between current state and the dream outcome.

Cover all of:
- **Technical** — what they don't know how to do
- **Emotional** — what they're afraid of, ashamed of, doubting
- **Practical** — time, money, energy constraints
- **Social** — what their spouse / boss / peer group will think
- **Logistical** — scheduling, location, dependencies
- **Identity** — "I'm not the kind of person who…"
- **Confidence** — proof they need before trusting the path

If you stop at 5 problems, you'll miss the one that's actually killing the sale. Push for 15+.

### Step 3 — Brainstorm Solutions per Problem
Divergent (this is why Ch 5 was the warmup). Many solutions per problem. Don't curate.

For each problem from Step 2, write **2–5 candidate solutions**. Wild ideas welcome. Quantity > quality at this stage.

### Step 4 — Run Each Solution Through the Delivery Cube
For every kept solution, decide *how* it gets delivered. Five dimensions:

| Dimension | Options |
|---|---|
| Level of personal attention | 1-on-1 / small group / one-to-many |
| Effort expected from customer | DIY / DWY / DFY |
| Live delivery medium | In-person / phone / email / text / Zoom / chat |
| Recorded delivery format | Audio / video / written |
| Response speed | 24/7 / 9–5 / within 5 min / within 1hr / within 24hr |

Same solution, different cube settings = different offer. Often the cheapest delivery method (one-to-many recorded video DIY) is dramatically worse than the most expensive (1-on-1 in-person DFY 24/7). The art is matching delivery method to where each solution actually moves the value equation.

### The 10× / 1/10th test (apply during Step 4)
For every solution, ask both:
- "If they paid 10× the current price, what would I add or change?"
- "If they paid 1/10th the current price, how would I still make them successful?"

Both directions surface delivery ideas you'd never reach by staying at the current price point.

### Step 5a — Trim
- Remove redundancies (two solutions doing the same job → pick one)
- Consolidate similar solutions (three calls/week + three videos + three guides → one program)
- Drop anything that doesn't materially move the value equation (Ch 4)

### Step 5b — Stack (assemble the offer)
- Sequence from first action the customer takes to the last outcome they achieve
- Create progression — each step earns the next
- Make the customer journey legible: "first you do X, then Y, then Z"
- This becomes the offer the customer actually experiences

## Diagnostic questions (ask the user)

Run the 5 steps as 5 distinct conversations, not one. Pause for user input between steps.

**Before Step 1:** "Let's confirm the dream outcome. From your avatar work, the implied dream is [pull from Ch 2]. Sound right? Want to refine?"

**Before Step 2:** "List every obstacle between [current state] and [dream outcome]. We're aiming for 15+ problems — cover technical, emotional, practical, social, logistical, identity, confidence. What's on your list?"

**Before Step 3:** "Now for each problem you listed, brainstorm 2–5 solutions. Wild ideas welcome — we'll filter in Step 5."

**Before Step 4:** "For each solution worth keeping, walk through the delivery cube. We'll also run the 10× / 1/10th test on the offer as a whole."

**Before Step 5:** "Trim time. Which solutions are redundant? Which should consolidate? Then we sequence what's left into the offer the customer will actually experience."

## Validation criteria (what "good" looks like)
- **Step 1:** Dream outcome is specific, time-bound, emotional, and avatar-aligned
- **Step 2:** ≥15 problems listed, covering all 7 problem categories
- **Step 3:** Average ≥2 solutions per problem (so ≥30 solutions for a 15-problem list)
- **Step 4:** Every kept solution has all 5 delivery-cube dimensions decided; 10×/1/10th test answered
- **Step 5:** Final offer has a clear customer journey (start → end), with redundancies removed

Push back if:
- Dream outcome is generic ("more customers", "better health") — push for specificity
- Fewer than 15 problems — they're missing the one that breaks the sale
- Solutions are all logical (more features, more time) — push for psychological + delivery innovations
- Delivery cube is filled in lazily ("Zoom, on-demand") for everything — different solutions need different cube settings

## Pitfalls (Hormozi's warnings)
- **"Solve them all."** "I can't tell you how many times one single item became the reason someone didn't buy. Solve them all." — Hormozi
- **Filtering during brainstorm.** Filter in Step 5. Premature filtering kills the best ideas.
- **Skipping the delivery cube.** Default delivery (Zoom + recordings) is rarely optimal. The cube forces a real choice per solution.
- **Stacking before trimming.** Sequence requires knowing what's in. Don't stack everything; trim first.
- **One-and-done.** After Ch 6 → run early cohort → ask non-buyers WHY → add solutions for their reasons. The travelling client who wouldn't buy fitness → add a travel plan → she bought.

## Workbook output expected
The completed 5-step core offer: dream outcome, 15+ problems, 30+ solutions, delivery cube per kept solution, trimmed and stacked into a final customer journey. This is the spine — the next 5 chapters wrap enhancers around it.
