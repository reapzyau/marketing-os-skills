# Chapter 7 — Scarcity

## Hormozi's why
Scarcity is a function of QUANTITY. Limited supply creates fear of missing out, and fear of loss is a stronger motivator than desire for gain. The musician who drops only 100 hoodies, never again, sells out — same hoodie at unlimited supply doesn't.

## Framework — three types of scarcity (services)

### Type 1: Total business cap
- Hard cap on total clients ever serviced. Period.
- Creates a waiting list. When a slot opens, people jump.
- Price resistance disappears at the top.
- "My agency services 25 customers total. Period."
- Best for: highest service tiers, white-glove offers.
- Periodically you can raise the cap 10–20% then close again.

### Type 2: Weekly / rolling cap
- X new clients per week. Rolling scarcity.
- Sales team has a recurring, legitimate urgency lever.
- "We only accept 5 new clients per week. Three spots are taken. I have 6 more calls this week — take a spot or one of my next calls will."

### Type 3: Cohort cap
- X clients per intake (monthly / quarterly).
- Operational benefits: choreographed onboarding, shared cohort experience.
- Dramatic open/close moments — "We take 100 clients 4 times a year."
- Best for: programs, courses, group coaching.

### Honest scarcity = always-on advantage
Almost every business is capacity-limited somehow. Just *tell* customers about it. "We're at 80% of capacity this week. If you want in, let me know now." Wins more business than the people who pretend they have unlimited capacity.

### Fast-money scarcity tip
Offer very limited 1-on-1 access to yourself — 5–10 people, communication method you don't hate (DM / email / voice memo / Zoom). Price 10–100× normal. Tell people. Announce when sold out.

## Diagnostic questions (ask the user)
Use `AskUserQuestion` here — this is a discrete choice.

**Question:** "Which scarcity type fits your business model?"
Options:
- **Total business cap** — premium tier, want a permanent waiting list
- **Weekly / rolling cap** — sales team needs recurring urgency lever
- **Cohort cap** — program/course, can choreograph intakes
- **None right now** — risky, ask why

Follow-up (open-ended):
- "What's the actual capacity number you can honor?"
- "How will you communicate when you sell out / hit the cap?"

## Validation criteria (what "good" looks like)
- ONE scarcity type chosen (not "we'll do all three")
- The cap number is **truthful** — they can actually deliver if they fill it
- A specific mechanism for announcing sellouts ("post in community", "email list update", "sales call script")
- Acknowledgement of the "must sell out" rule — they understand they cannot exceed the cap

Push back if:
- Cap is set arbitrarily high to feel "abundant" — that's not scarcity
- Cap is fake ("we'll say 100 but accept 200 if they ask") — this destroys long-term trust
- Multiple types chosen — pick ONE primary; others muddy the signal

## Pitfalls (Hormozi's warnings)
- **THE INVIOLABLE RULE.** "If you say 100 spots and let 110 in, you lose all trust forever." There is no recovery from broken scarcity. Honor the cap.
- **Fake scarcity = short-term win, long-term death.** The audience figures it out.
- **Picking multiple scarcity types.** Confuses the signal. Pick ONE primary.
- **Not announcing sellouts.** When you sell out, TELL everyone. That's the marketing — it earns the next cohort.

## Workbook output expected
