# Chapter 9 — Bonuses

## Hormozi's why
A single offer is worth less than the same offer broken into component parts and stacked as bonuses. "But wait — there's more!" works because each bonus expands the price-to-value gap until the prospect feels stupid saying no.

## Framework — 15 bonus tactics (apply as many as fit)

1. **Always offer bonuses.** You won't sell less because of them.
2. **Name them with the benefit in the title.** Not "Bonus 1" — "The Same-Day Lead Reactivation Playbook."
3. **Tell the full story** for each bonus: what it is, how you created it, why it relates to their issue, how it improves their life, what it makes faster/easier.
4. **Provide proof** for each — a stat, a client, a personal experience.
5. **Paint the vivid mental picture** of life with the bonus already used.
6. **Always ascribe a price tag** — "Normally $500", "3 months to build, worth $2,000". Justify it.
7. **Tools and checklists beat more trainings.** Lower effort = higher value-equation score.
8. **Each bonus tackles a specific objection** the prospect would otherwise raise.
9. **Solve their NEXT problem before they hit it** — what's the obvious thing they'll need *after* succeeding with the core offer?
10. **Bonuses should eclipse the core offer in stated value.** Subconscious effect: the core must be even more valuable if these are mere bonuses.
11. **Invest once, give endless times** — templates, swipe files, scripts, checklists. High creation cost, zero marginal cost.
12. **Record everything** — workshops, webinars, events, interviews. Free perpetual bonuses.
13. **Promote sizzle to bonus, demote substance to core.** A flashy checklist can be the "bonus" even if it's central to the work.
14. **Stack scarcity / urgency on bonuses.** "3 tickets to next event included if you buy this week."
15. **Make the guarantee itself a bonus.** "Move today, I'll add a 30-day money-back guarantee I don't normally offer."

## The OPP (Other People's Products) play
Non-competitive businesses give free samples / discounts as bonuses. They get exposure to your buyers; you get bonuses for nothing.

### Worked example: pain clinic
- Massage therapist → 1–2 free massages
- Chiropractor → 2 free adjustments ($100 value)
- Low-inflammation food company → $50 in discounts
- Orthotics company → $150 in discounts
- Gym → free PT session + free month of pool ($100)
- Pharmacy → $100/mo in drug discounts

Stack enough of these and the bonuses alone exceed the program price. You can literally sell the program on the bonuses.

### Advanced — get *paid* for the bonuses
Charge OPP partners per introduction or % of sales. A $400 offer with 5 partners paying $100/intro = +$500 profit per customer at zero added cost.

## Diagnostic questions (ask the user)

Brainstorm-heavy chapter — use conversation:

1. "From Ch 6's solutions and Ch 5's bricks: which 5–10 components should we promote to *bonus* status?"
2. "For each bonus, what specific OBJECTION does it kill?"
3. "What's the prospect's NEXT problem after the core offer succeeds? Can we include a bonus that solves it?"
4. "Are there 2–3 OPP partners we could approach for free/discounted goods to bundle in?"
5. "What's the dollar value we'll ascribe to each bonus, and what's the math behind that number?"

## Validation criteria (what "good" looks like)
- ≥5 bonuses (≥7 if the offer is high-ticket)
- Each bonus has: benefit-in-title name, story, proof, price tag, the objection it kills
- Total bonus value ≥ core offer price (Tactic 10 — eclipse the core)
- At least 1 "solve next problem" bonus (Tactic 9)
- At least 1 OPP partnership identified (or explicit decision not to pursue)
- At least 1 tool/checklist bonus (Tactic 7) — not just more video training

Push back if:
- All bonuses are "more of the same" (more videos, more calls) — the value equation hates added effort. Push for tools, scripts, templates instead.
- Bonus names are generic ("Bonus 1: Workbook") — every bonus needs a benefit-in-title name
- Stated values are made-up ("worth $5,000!") with no math — push for honest pricing logic
- No objection mapping — bonuses must be objection-killers, not decoration

## Pitfalls (Hormozi's warnings)
- **Adding effort instead of subtracting it.** Bonuses that say "another 4 hours of training" make the offer *worse* on the value equation. Templates and checklists win.
- **Made-up prices.** If the stated value is implausible, it discredits the whole offer. Justify each price tag.
- **Discounting instead of bonusing.** "Once you've stated the price, never discount." Discounts shrink margin. Bonuses expand value.
- **Treating bonuses as decoration.** Each one should kill a specific objection. If you can't name the objection it kills, the bonus probably shouldn't be there.

## Workbook output expected
A 5–10 bonus stack with names, stories, proof, ascribed values (totalling ≥ core price), the specific objection each kills, at least one "solve next problem" bonus, and either OPP partners identified or an explicit decision not to pursue them this round.
