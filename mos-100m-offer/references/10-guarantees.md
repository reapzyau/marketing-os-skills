# Chapter 10 — Guarantees

## Hormozi's why
The greatest objection to any purchase is RISK. Reverse the risk and conversion can 2–4×. Even after refund rates rise, the math still works heavily in your favor. Experienced marketers spend as much time on guarantees as on the deliverable itself.

## Framework — anatomy of a guarantee with teeth

A real guarantee is a **conditional statement** with consequences:

> "If you do not get [X result] in [Y time period], we will [Z action]."

Without the "Z" (the consequence), the guarantee has no teeth. It's decoration.

### Bad guarantee (no teeth)
"We will get you 20 clients guaranteed." → What if you don't? Nothing happens. Useless.

### Good guarantee (has teeth)
"You will get 20 clients in your first 30 days, or we give you your money back PLUS your advertising dollars spent with us." → Specific, time-bound, consequence stated.

### The guarantee math (why this works even when refund rate doubles)
- Add guarantee → 30% more closes
- Refund rate goes 5% → 10% (doubles)
- Old: 100 sales − 5 refunds = 95 paid customers
- New: 130 sales − 13 refunds = 117 paid customers
- Net: **+23% paid customers**

For the guarantee to NOT be worth it, the sales lift would need to be 100%-offset by refunds. Almost never happens.

## The four types of guarantees

### Type 1 — Unconditional
"No questions asked." Most powerful. Use when:
- Delivery cost is low
- Margin is high
- You can absorb refunds
Avoid when delivery is expensive or work is bespoke.

### Type 2 — Conditional
"Refund IF you did the work and still didn't get the result."
- Protects you from non-doers
- Slightly less psychological lift than unconditional
- Safest option for high-touch services

### Type 3 — Anti-guarantee
Guarantee your INPUTS, not the outcome.
- "I'll personally call you every Monday for 12 weeks, or you don't pay."
- Can't lose — you either call or you don't.
- Useful when outcome is partly out of your control (the prospect must do their part).

### Type 4 — Implied
No formal guarantee, but state WHY you're confident:
- "I've done this 10,000 times."
- "500+ documented case studies."
- "This makes us $10M/yr — we know it works."
- Use when explicit guarantees are legally risky or the offer is too custom.

## Building the guarantee — the 7-step process

1. **List the client's biggest fears** about your offer. (What don't they want to happen?)
2. **Reverse each fear into a guarantee clause.**
3. **Add time, emotion, outside cost** — "if you haven't experienced X" / "if you don't save Y".
4. **Be specific & creative.** Vague kills conviction.
5. **Avoid unconditional** if your delivery is expensive — use conditional / anti / implied.
6. **Explain the guarantee BOLDLY.** Even if you don't offer one, explain *why*.
7. **Stack multiple guarantees** with different timelines: "30 days you'll have X, 60 days Y, 90 days Z."

## Name the guarantee (don't be generic)
"30 Day Money Back Satisfaction Guarantee" = vanilla. Forgotten.
"Club a Baby Seal Guarantee — after 30 days, if you wouldn't club a baby seal to stay on as a customer, you don't pay a penny" = memorable.

The name does psychological work. Make it specific and creative.

## Diagnostic questions (ask the user)

1. "What are the 5–10 biggest fears your prospect has BEFORE buying? Make a list."
2. "For each top fear, what's the guarantee that would eliminate it?"
3. (`AskUserQuestion`) "Which guarantee type fits your business?"
   - Unconditional — low delivery cost, high margin
   - Conditional — high-touch service, protect against non-doers
   - Anti-guarantee — outcome partly out of your control
   - Implied — explicit guarantees too risky / offer too custom
4. "Should we stack 2–3 guarantees on different timelines? (30/60/90)"
5. "Name your guarantee — make it memorable, not vanilla."

## Validation criteria (what "good" looks like)
- The guarantee is a **conditional statement** with explicit Z consequence — not "satisfaction guaranteed"
- One guarantee type chosen, matched to delivery cost / business model
- At least 3 prospect fears listed and each addressed in the guarantee
- The guarantee has a SPECIFIC name — not "30-Day Money Back"
- Optionally: 2–3 stacked guarantees on different timelines (more powerful, more risk to manage)
- The guarantee math has been mentally checked (lift > refund)

Push back if:
- "Satisfaction guaranteed" with no consequence → not a guarantee
- Unconditional chosen on high-cost custom work → risky, suggest conditional
- Generic name → push for memorable / creative
- Guarantee doesn't tie to a real prospect fear → it's decoration, not risk reversal

## Pitfalls (Hormozi's warnings)
- **No teeth.** A guarantee without a stated consequence is decoration. Always have a "Z".
- **Vague consequences.** "We'll make it right" → meaningless. Be specific: refund + ad spend + a phone apology, whatever it is.
- **Unconditional on high-cost custom work.** You can go bankrupt before learning the lesson. Use conditional / anti / implied.
- **Generic naming.** "30-Day Money Back Satisfaction Guarantee" is forgotten in 5 seconds. Name it creatively.
- **Not explaining the guarantee.** Even when you have a great guarantee, prospects miss it. Explain it BOLDLY in the offer presentation.

## Workbook output expected
