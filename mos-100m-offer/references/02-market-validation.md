# Chapter 2 — Market Validation (Starving Crowd)

## Hormozi's why
"Market > Offer > Persuasion." A Grand Slam Offer to the wrong market falls on deaf ears. In COVID, toilet paper sold for $100/roll with no offer and no persuasion — because the audience was starving. Find a starving crowd before you build anything.

## Framework — IMPORTANT: this skill DOES NOT rebuild the avatar
The user already has avatar work (from `/mos-avatar` or in `reference/core/audience.md`). The job here is **fast 4-tenet validation** of the existing avatar — not a rebuild.

If avatar is missing entirely → tell the user to run `/mos-avatar` first and stop. Do not attempt to construct an avatar from scratch in this chapter.

### The 4 tenets of a good market
A market must satisfy ALL FOUR — not most, all four. Score each 1–5 against the existing avatar.

1. **Massive pain.** The pain must be acute enough that hearing your solution makes them lean in. Pain proportional to price you can charge. "No pain, no premium."
2. **Purchasing power.** They must have money or access to money at your price point. The resume coach selling to unemployed people learns this the hard way.
3. **Easy to target.** They must congregate somewhere reachable — channel, list, community, geography. "Anyone with the problem" is not a target.
4. **Growing.** Tailwind beats headwind. Fertility services grow. Newspapers shrink 25%/year. You don't need a great market — you need a non-bad one.

If any tenet scores ≤2 → surface it as a blocker. The user should consider repositioning the offer or picking a different niche before continuing.

### Niche depth check (optional second pass)
"Riches in the niches." Same product, named for a narrower audience, sells for 100× more:
- L1: Time Management → $19
- L2: Time Management for Sales Professionals → $99
- L3: Time Management for Outbound B2B Sales → $499
- L4: Time Management for Outbound B2B Power-Tools Sales Reps → $1,997

Ask: where is the existing avatar on this ladder? If L1 or L2, suggest going deeper before naming the offer (Ch 11). If L3 or L4, proceed.

## Diagnostic questions (ask the user)
1. "Walk me through the existing avatar in 2–3 sentences — who is this person, what's their world?"
2. "On the 4 tenets, what's your gut score for each (Pain / Purchasing Power / Targetable / Growing)? Be honest about the weakest one."
3. "How deep is the avatar on the niche ladder (L1–L4)? Is there room to go narrower without losing volume?"

## Validation criteria (what "good" looks like)
- All 4 tenets explicitly scored 1–5 against the actual avatar (not aspirational)
- The weakest tenet is named and either (a) accepted as a known risk or (b) flagged as a blocker to revisit
- Niche depth identified — L3 or deeper for premium pricing to work
- A 1-line "starving crowd statement": *"[Avatar] is starving for [outcome] and willing to pay [price range] because [pain]."*

Push back if:
- Pain score ≥4 but the user can't articulate the specific pain → probably aspirational, not real
- Purchasing power assumed without evidence — ask "what's the avatar currently paying for adjacent solutions?"
- The "starving crowd statement" sounds like marketing — Hormozi-good means it's literal and specific

## Pitfalls (Hormozi's warnings)
- **Hopping niches.** Picking dentists, then bouncing to chiropractors after one rough quarter. Both are fine markets — but pick ONE and stay until you crack it.
- **Wishing for great instead of accepting good.** You don't need a great market. You need a not-bad one. Don't sit waiting for the perfect avatar; validate the one you have.
- **Pricing power without purchasing power.** Acute pain + no money = no business. Verify spend before going deeper.
