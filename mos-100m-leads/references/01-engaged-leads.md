# Chapter 1 — Engaged Leads (The Reframe)

## Hormozi's why
Most entrepreneurs think about advertising the wrong way — so they do the wrong things to get leads. Fix the words and you fix the thinking. The goal is not "leads," it's **engaged leads**: people who actually SHOW interest. Those are the only ones that matter — "engaged leads are the true output of advertising."

## Framework — the definitions that change behaviour
- **Lead** = a person you can contact. That's all. A name in your phone, a bought email, someone on the street.
- **Engaged lead** = a person who *shows* interest in what you sell — opts in on a site, replies to your campaign, follows you and is contactable, books a call, sends a DM.
- **Advertising** = "the process of making known." Letting people who don't know about your stuff find out it exists.
- **The growth chain:** more people know → more buy → more money. You only grow two ways: (1) get more customers, (2) make each worth more. This skill is lever #1 — more, better, cheaper, more-reliable engaged leads.
- **The premise to accept:** "All else equal, when you double your engaged leads, you double your business."

## Diagnostic questions (ask the user)
Mostly open, but anchor on a number:
1. "What are you counting as a 'lead' right now — a click, a name, a reply?"
2. "Of those, how many actually *showed interest* (engaged) versus just sitting on a list?"
3. "What's your current count of engaged leads per week — and is it tracked anywhere?"
4. "Is your real problem that potential customers are *ignorant of your existence* — i.e. you're under-advertising?"

The chapter's output: a single, measurable engaged-lead metric and a weekly target.

## Validation criteria (what "good" looks like)
- The user states a concrete, measurable engaged-lead number (or commits to start tracking one)
- They distinguish engaged leads from raw contacts / impressions / followers
- They set a weekly engaged-lead TARGET tied to the offer's funnel math (pulled from the offer workbook)

Push back if:
- They conflate impressions / clicks / followers with engagement — those aren't engaged leads
- "We get some interest" — too vague; force a number
- No tracking exists — the first action is to start counting

## Pitfalls (Hormozi's warnings)
- Treating any contact as a "lead" and being satisfied
- Trying to "skip to the make-money part" before fixing the lead engine
- Under-advertising and blaming the market
- Measuring vanity metrics (impressions, followers) instead of engaged leads

## Workbook output expected
A single engaged-lead definition + a tracked current number + a weekly target tied to the offer's funnel math. This metric governs the rest of the plan.
