# Chapter 10 — Open To Goal + The Roadmap

## Hormozi's why
"Most people dramatically underestimate the volume it takes to make advertising work." Advertising is an inputs→outputs game and the one rule is volume. Then a sequenced roadmap scales you from first leads to a $100M machine. "You cannot lose if you do not quit."

## Framework
**Open To Goal (the one rule):** the Rule of 100 is the floor; Open To Goal is "Rule of 100 on steroids" — commit to the WORK until you hit a specific OUTCOME, no matter how long it takes (e.g. "sign 5 members/day, however long it takes"). Habit stack: wake early, get straight to work (no rituals), no meetings until noon — uninterrupted work right after uninterrupted sleep.

**One-Page Advertising Checklist (5 steps):**
1. Pick the engaged-lead type: Customers, Affiliates, Employees, or Agencies.
2. Pick Rule of 100 or Open To Goal; commit to daily actions.
3. Fill out the advertising checklist for that daily action.
4. Do it daily until you can afford to pay someone else to do it.
5. Then restart at step 1 with **employees** as the target lead type; repeat → build the team → scale again.

**The Roadmap (levels, $0 → $100M+):**
- **L1:** Friends know your stuff — one offer, one avatar, one platform. *Action: warm outreach.*
- **L2:** You consistently tell everyone + know your inputs→leads, maxing personal capacity. *Action: warm outreach + posting content.*
- **L3:** Hire employees to do more advertising profitably for you.
- **L4:** Product good enough for consistent referrals (target ≥25%). Build goodwill + a referral program *before* scaling spend. ("Where most people mess up — they let the product slip.")
- **L5:** More places / ways / people — new audiences & placements, then add a platform / lead-getter / Core Four activity. *Action: ≥2 methods on multiple platforms.*
- **L6:** Hire executives who run advertising departments without you.
- **L7:** "I'll edit this once I cross a billion."

**A Decade in a Page / "Many-Sided Die":** keep rolling (advertising) and the greens compound. Two guarantees: "the more you roll, the better you get" and "if you quit, you lose."

## Diagnostic questions (ask the user)
1. "Which Roadmap level are you at — and what's that level's primary action?"
2. "Are you working to a *number of outcomes* (Open To Goal) or just 'doing your best'?"
3. "What does your one-page checklist say — lead type, method, daily action?"
4. "Are you doing enough volume, or a fraction of what's required?"
5. "Is your product good enough for ≥25% referrals before you scale spend (the Level 4 trap)?"

## Validation criteria (what "good" looks like)
- A correctly self-identified Roadmap level + the level-appropriate action
- A single-page plan with a committed daily Open-To-Goal action + a realistic 5-10yr horizon
- A budget slice reserved for testing (permission to fail)

Push back if:
- "I did the right stuff but it didn't work" (with low volume); "doing my best" instead of to-goal
- Expecting overnight success; scaling spend while the product slips

## Pitfalls (Hormozi's warnings)
- Underestimating required volume; "doing your best" not "what's required"
- Get-rich-quick switching after a few losses; letting the product slip at Level 4
- A 100-page plan that never gets used (use the one-pager); quitting

## Workbook output expected
The execution commitment: current Roadmap level + action, an Open-To-Goal daily target, a one-page checklist, a sequenced roadmap of next moves, a testing budget, and a realistic multi-year horizon.
