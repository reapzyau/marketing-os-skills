# Chapter 3 — The Core Four (Choose Your Primary Channel)

## Hormozi's why
There are only FOUR ways one person can let other people know about anything. If you're not getting enough leads, you're not doing the Core Four with enough skill or volume. Master ONE and you have a lifelong "get out of jail free" card.

## Framework — the 2×2 + how to choose
**Two audiences** × **two modes:**

| | 1-to-1 (private) | 1-to-many (public) |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm** (gave you permission) | **Warm Outreach** | **Post Free Content** |
| **Cold** (strangers) | **Cold Outreach** | **Paid Ads** |

- Automation doesn't change the *nature* of the communication (emailing 10,000 people is still "1-to-1, fast, by a machine").
- **How to choose where to start:** do warm outreach first to seed customers. Then — **more time than money → post content; more money than time → cold outreach or paid ads.** You only need ONE to get all the engaged leads you want. The methods compound — each makes the others more effective.

## Diagnostic questions (ask the user)
**Q1 (open):** "Right now — do you have more time, or more money?"
**Q2 (open):** "Which of the Core Four are you already doing, if any? Have you done warm outreach to seed your first customers?"
**Q3 (AskUserQuestion — the commitment):** "Which ONE channel is your primary, to master first?"
Options:
- **Warm Outreach** — 1-to-1 to people who know you. Cheapest, most reliable, best for first clients.
- **Post Free Content** — 1-to-many to a warm audience. Best if you have more time than money; compounds.
- **Cold Outreach** — 1-to-1 to strangers. Best if you'll grind; you get to pick your targets.
- **Paid Ads** — 1-to-many to strangers. Fastest reach; needs budget + tracking.

The choice determines which channel chapter (4–7) gets the deep build. The other three are deferred.

## Validation criteria (what "good" looks like)
- ONE primary channel chosen, matching the user's real time-vs-money situation
- Warm outreach acknowledged as the seeding step (even if the primary is something else)
- A commitment to volume on the one channel before adding another

Push back if:
- They want to do all four at once — that's the spread-thin trap; pick one
- They skip warm outreach when they have no customers yet
- They blame "the market is saturated" rather than low skill / low volume

## Pitfalls (Hormozi's warnings)
- Spreading thin across all four instead of maxing one
- Confusing automation with a different "type" of advertising
- Not doing enough volume; switching methods after a few losses
- Skipping warm outreach as the seed

## Workbook output expected
ONE primary channel committed, matched to the user's time/money reality, with the other three explicitly deferred. This selection drives the deep build in the next chapter.
