# Chapter 2 — The Lead Magnet (The Heart)

## Hormozi's why
Don't advertise your core offer to strangers — advertise a **lead magnet**: a complete solution to a *narrow* problem, free or low-cost, that turns strangers into engaged leads. A person who pays with their *time* now is far more likely to pay with their *money* later. A great lead magnet gets more engaged leads and customers than the core offer alone, for less money. Step 0: "If you're struggling to get leads, make an amazing lead magnet."

## Framework — definitions + the 7 steps (do in order)

**Definitions:**
- **Core offer** = the main thing you sell for money (it's in the offer workbook). Try advertising it directly first.
- **Lead magnet** = a complete solution to a NARROW problem. Low-cost/free. Once solved, it *reveals* the next problem — which your core offer solves. (Salty pretzels → reveal thirst → buy a drink.) It should be valuable enough that you *could* charge for it.

This chapter is SEVEN sub-conversations, not one. Pause between sub-steps. This is the most important chapter in the skill — a mediocre lead magnet sinks the whole plan.

### Step 1 — Pick the narrow problem + who it's for
Use the **Problem-Solution cycle**: every problem has a solution; every solution reveals a new problem. Pick a problem that's NARROW and MEANINGFUL, solve it, and make sure your *core offer* solves the next revealed problem. (Selling a home: valuation, photos, cleaning, staging are all narrow problems → the core offer sells the home.)

### Step 2 — Pick the TYPE (3 types)
1. **Reveal Their Problem** ("diagnosis") — best when the problem worsens the longer you wait. (Website speed test, posture analysis, termite inspection.)
2. **Sample / Trial** — full but brief access to the core offer; limit uses, time, or both. Best when the core offer is a recurring solution to a recurring problem. (Costco food samples.)
3. **One Step of a Multi-Step Process** — give one valuable step free, the rest on purchase. Best for complex core offers. (This book itself; the first coat of three.)

### Step 3 — Pick the DELIVERY (4 methods)
1. **Software** — a tool (calculator, spreadsheet, dashboard).
2. **Information** — teach something (mini-course, lessons, event).
3. **Services** — do work for free (audit, run their ads 30 days).
4. **Physical Product** — something they hold (book, sample, assessment chart).

3 types × 4 delivery methods = up to **12 versions** of one lead magnet. Make several and rotate to keep advertising fresh and low-effort.

### Step 4 — Test the NAME / headline
Ogilvy: the headline is "80 cents of your advertising dollar" — 5× more people read it. Test in order: **headline → image(s) → subheadline**. Run polls; you don't need many votes for direction. (Bonus signal: people asking *when* they can get it = mega winner.)

### Step 5 — Make it EASY to consume
Less effort = 2-4× take rates. Offer every format: software on phone + desktop; information as video/text/audio; services more times/ways/channels; physical simple to order, fast to arrive, easy to open, simple directions.

### Step 6 — Make it DARN GOOD ("Give away the secrets, sell the implementation")
Provide more value than the COST of your core offer *before* they buy. 99% won't buy — but they judge your reputation on the free stuff. Make the lead magnet as good as your paid stuff. No "sucky fluff" — it actively destroys reputation.

### Step 7 — The CTA (make it easy to say "I want more")
Two parts: **(1) what to do** (clear, simple, direct — "call now," not "don't delay") and **(2) reasons to act now:**
- **a) Scarcity** — limited supply vs demand. Use REAL/ethical scarcity (your true capacity: "I can only take five a week").
- **b) Urgency** — limited time; real deadlines, expiring bonuses.
- **c) "Make up a reason" (the 'because' effect)** — any reason beats none ("…because it's my birthday"). Put real content after the word *because*.

"Don't be clever, be clear."

## Diagnostic questions (ask the user)
Run the 7 steps as 7 short sub-conversations. Tie back to the offer workbook throughout.
1. (Step 1) "What narrow, meaningful problem can you solve for free that *reveals* the problem your core offer fixes?"
2. (Step 2 — AskUserQuestion) "Which type fits: Reveal Their Problem / Sample-Trial / One Step of a Multi-Step?"
3. (Step 3 — AskUserQuestion) "Which delivery: Software / Information / Services / Physical?"
4. (Step 4) "What's the headline/name — and what will you test it against?"
5. (Step 5) "Which formats will you package it in?"
6. (Step 6) "How does this deliver more value than your core offer's price, before they pay?"
7. (Step 7) "What's the CTA + your real scarcity + a 'because' reason?"

## Validation criteria (what "good" looks like)
- Solves ONE narrow problem, and the **next revealed problem maps directly to the core offer**
- Genuinely valuable enough to charge for (not fluff)
- A type + delivery chosen (ideally several versions to rotate)
- A headline chosen with a plan to test it
- A CTA with a clear *what* + a *why* (scarcity / urgency / reason)

Push back if:
- The magnet is broad/vague, or doesn't connect to the core offer
- It's "sucky fluff" — worse than nothing (reputation damage)
- The headline is untested
- The CTA has no urgency or reason to act now
- Scarcity is faked rather than a real capacity limit

## Pitfalls (Hormozi's warnings)
- Problem too broad; or the revealed problem doesn't connect to the core offer
- Giving away "sucky fluff" — worse than giving nothing
- Not testing the name/headline (80% of the impact)
- Only one format; being clever instead of clear in the CTA
- Faking scarcity instead of using a real limit

## Workbook output expected
A complete, named lead magnet: narrow problem, type, delivery (with rotation versions), tested headline, packaging, an over-delivering value rationale, and a CTA with real scarcity/urgency/reason. This is the centerpiece the whole channel plan advertises.
