# Chapter 7 — Run Paid Ads

> Build in depth ONLY if Paid Ads was chosen as the primary channel in Ch 3. Otherwise note it as a deferred channel and move on.

## Hormozi's why
Paid ads (1-to-many to strangers) are the fastest way to get the most people to see your stuff — you trade money for reach. It's a game of *efficiency*, not creativity: "All advertising works. The only thing that differs is how well."

## Framework

**Part I — make the ad (4 steps + the 3-chunk formula):**
- **Step 1: Pick a platform** where 4 things are true — you've used it and got value; you can target your people; you know the ad format; you have the minimum budget.
- **Step 2: Target** — **lookalike audience** (upload your best-customer list; if too small, add warm then cold lists to hit the minimum) + **targeting factors** (age/income/gender/interests/time/location). More filters = more efficient but burn faster; start specific to win early, then broaden.
- **Step 3: The ad = Call Out + Value + CTA:**
  - **Call Out** (most important — front-load the effort; "cocktail party effect"). Verbal: **Labels** (e.g. "Clark County Moms" — local beats general), **Yes-Questions**, **If-Then Statements**, **Ridiculous Results**. Nonverbal: **Contrast**, **Likeness**, **The Scene**.
  - **Value — the What-Who-When framework:** *What* = the 4 value drivers (Dream Outcome, Perceived Likelihood ↑; Time Delay, Effort & Sacrifice ↓) **plus their 4 opposites** (Nightmare, Risk, slow trajectory, more work); *Who* = status across the people they know; *When* = past/present/future. Long vs short ads differ only in how many angles they cover.
  - **CTA** — spell out the next action; make it quick/easy (short memorable domain); layer urgency/scarcity/bonuses.
- **Step 4: Get permission** — a simple landing page that MATCHES the ad ("continuous experience from click to close"; use consistency).

**Part II — money stuff:**
- **How much to spend — 3 phases:** (1) **Track money** (set up tracking before spending a cent); (2) **Lose money** (budget 2× the 30-day cash a customer brings to test an ad; kill before 1× if zero leads; expect nine losers, one winner, then 100× it); (3) **Print money** (reverse-budget from how many customers you can handle; +20% pad).
- **How well am I doing — LTGP : CAC.** Use **LTGP** (lifetime gross profit = all revenue − cost to deliver), not "LTV." Aim **≥ 3:1.** Below 3× the industry-average CAC → fix the business model (raise LTGP). Above 3× → fix advertising (lower CAC).
- **If ads aren't profitable → Client-Financed Acquisition:** make the customer pay back cost-to-get + cost-to-fulfil within the **first 30 days** (add an upsell). Then recycle cash → limitless scale.

## Diagnostic questions (ask the user)
1. "Which platform meets all four requirements for you?"
2. "What lists can you upload to build a lookalike audience?"
3. "What's your callout — is the first 5 seconds doing the heavy lifting?"
4. "What's your LTGP and CAC — is the ratio above 3:1?"
5. "Can the customer pay back acquisition + fulfilment within 30 days — what upsell makes that true?"

## Validation criteria (what "good" looks like)
- Tracking in place; a platform + lookalike + filters
- An ad with a sharp callout + What-Who-When value + an explicit CTA; a matching landing page
- LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1 with a 30-day client-financed payback plan

Push back if:
- Obsessing over "creative" instead of the numbers; no tracking
- No lookalike; weak callout; unknown LTGP/CAC; no 30-day payback
- Confusing a sales problem with an advertising problem

## Pitfalls (Hormozi's warnings)
- Spending before tracking; giving up too early (or letting losers run too long)
- Confusing a business-model (low LTGP) problem with a bad-ads (high CAC) problem
- Confusing a sales problem with an advertising problem; ad↔landing-page mismatch ("Frankenstein experience")

## Workbook output expected
A paid-ads plan: platform + lookalike + filters, an ad (callout + What-Who-When + CTA), a matching landing page, a budget phase + test budget, LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1, and a 30-day client-financed payback.
