# Kennedy System — The Ultimate Sales Letter (Distilled Framework)

Source: Dan Kennedy, *The Ultimate Sales Letter* (4th Edition)
Purpose: Actionable copywriting reference for sales messages in any medium.

---

## Core Principle

A sales letter is NOT creative writing. It is a mechanical assembly process — gathering proven components and arranging them in a reliable framework. Preparation is more than half the battle. Writing is the easy part.

**"Sales letter" vs "sales message":** Kennedy uses "sales letter" to mean any persuasive piece — email, landing page, DM, video script, ad. The principles are medium-agnostic. Only delivery mechanics change.

---

## THE 29-STEP SYSTEM

### PHASE 1: Research & Raw Material (Steps 1-3)

#### Step 1: Get Into the Customer
APPLY WHEN: Before writing anything. This is non-negotiable.

Use Kennedy's "10 Smart Market Diagnosis Questions":
1. What keeps them awake at night?
2. What are they afraid of?
3. What are they angry about? Who are they angry at?
4. What are their top 3 daily frustrations?
5. What trends are occurring in their business/life?
6. What do they secretly, ardently desire most?
7. Is there a built-in bias in how they make decisions?
8. Do they have their own language/jargon?
9. Who else is selling something similar, and how?
10. Who else has tried selling them something similar, and failed?

**Collier Principle:** "Always enter the conversation already occurring in the customer's mind." Don't interrupt — align with what they're already thinking and talking about, then steer toward your message.

TEMPLATE: Write a one-paragraph "day in the life" of your reader. What did they wake up worrying about? What are they talking about at the water cooler? What news story has them fired up? Start your message THERE.

#### Step 2: Get Into the Offer
APPLY WHEN: Alongside Step 1. Use the product. Analyze every angle.

- Build a list of every FEATURE on separate cards/lines
- Translate each feature into a BENEFIT (people buy what things DO, not what things ARE)
- Look for the "Hidden Benefit" — not the obvious one, but one of profound importance that's easy to miss
- Sort by importance to the reader (not to you)

TEMPLATE — Feature/Benefit translation:
```
Feature: [What it is/has]
Obvious Benefit: [What it does]
Hidden Benefit: [What that MEANS for their life/feelings/status]
```

#### Step 3: Create a Damaging Admission
APPLY WHEN: Your offer has obvious flaws, you're in a competitive market, or prospects are skeptical.

**What it is:** Openly acknowledge the weaknesses, flaws, or limitations of your offer. This builds massive credibility because "nothing's perfect and everybody knows that."

**Why it works:** When you admit a flaw FIRST, everything else you say becomes more believable. The reader thinks: "If they're honest about THAT, the good stuff must be true too."


TEMPLATE:
```
"If you want [impressive thing competitor offers], our [product] is not for you.
But if you mostly want [the thing that actually matters]
and will trade [the fancy thing] for [your honest alternative],
you'll like [your offer] just fine."
```

Turn competitive disadvantages INTO the selling story.

### PHASE 2: Delivery & Attention (Steps 4-6)

#### Steps 4-5: Get Delivered, Get Looked At
APPLY WHEN: Any medium where you compete for attention (subject lines, ad creative, envelope).

- "Sneak-up" = plain sender, no branding, conversational (for cold prospects)
- "Billboard" = full promotional, curiosity/benefit-led (for existing customers)
- Rule: never be half-pregnant. Either disguise as personal OR go full promotional.
- Magic words (adapt to subject lines): "Photo enclosed," "[Topic] alert for [group]," "Your [thing] enclosed"

#### Step 6: Get Your Sales Letter Read
APPLY WHEN: Writing the headline/opening. This is your "pest to welcome guest" moment.

**Headline is mandatory.** Even in a "letter" format.

**Proven headline templates:**
- "They Didn't Think I Could ________, but I Did."
- "Who Else Wants ________?"
- "How ________ Made Me ________"
- "Are You ________?"
- "How to ________"
- "Secrets Of ________"
- "Warning: ________"
- "Give Me ________ and I'll ________"
- "________ Ways to ________"
- "Thousands Now ________ Even Though They ________"

**Flagging** — Attach a "who is this for?" flag to any headline:
- Audience flag: "Agency Owners: [headline]"
- Problem flag: "Losing clients to AI? [headline]"

### PHASE 3: Persuasion Architecture (Steps 7-8)

#### Step 7: Beat the Bugaboo
APPLY WHEN: Price is a factor, or any significant objection exists. "Bugaboo" = the scary thing that stops the sale.

**What it is:** Find an enemy/villain for the reader to blame for their problem. Give them something external to be angry at — then position yourself as their ally against it.

**Why it works:** People don't want to blame themselves. An external enemy creates us-vs-them solidarity between you and the reader. It channels negative emotion (anger, resentment, fear) toward action rather than inaction.

**The Bugaboo Pattern:**
1. Name the enemy (big tech, the government, gurus, their industry's "old guard," etc.)
2. Show how the enemy is responsible for the reader's pain
3. Position yourself as someone who's ALSO been a victim of / fighting against this enemy
4. Present your offer as the weapon/escape/solution

**Price minimization strategies:**
- Sell bulk (emphasize volume/quantity of what they get)
- Discuss the cost to develop it (R&D, years of testing)
- Make the parts worth more than the whole (itemize value of each component)
- Conceal in payments ("3 easy payments of $19.95" vs "$59.85")
- Demonstrate ROI — "sell money at a discount"

#### Step 8: Motivate Action — Three Core Formulas

**Formula 1: Problem-Agitation-Solution (P-A-S)**
APPLY WHEN: You're solving a painful problem. This is the single most reliable sales formula ever invented.

1. **Problem** — State it clearly. Just enough to get agreement.
2. **Agitate** — Stir up emotional response. Make the problem WORSE. Tap anger, fear, embarrassment, guilt. "Make them see the hearse backed up to the door."
3. **Solution** — Unveil your offer as the answer.

TEMPLATE:
```
[PROBLEM] You know that feeling when [specific pain point]...
[AGITATE] And the worst part? [Make it worse — consequences, what happens if they don't act, how others are laughing/winning/stealing from them]
[SOLUTION] That's exactly why [your offer] exists. Here's how it works...
```

**Formula 2: Fortune-Telling**
APPLY WHEN: You can make credible predictions about trends, changes, or future events.

Predict what's coming. People are fascinated by predictions. Then position your product as preparation for that future.

**Formula 3: Winners and Losers**
APPLY WHEN: You want to create fear of being left behind and desire to join the successful group.

"Take any 100 [people like the reader]... X% end up [winning result]. Y% end up [losing result]. The difference? [Your product/approach]."

### PHASE 4: Writing & Rewriting (Steps 9-11)

#### Step 9: Write the First Draft
Just write. Don't edit. Expect 2-3x the final length. Get everything on the table.

#### Step 10: Rewrite for Strategy

**The Yes Sequence:**
APPLY WHEN: Throughout the letter, especially the opening.

Give the reader a series of questions/statements they naturally agree with. This builds "yes momentum" — a pattern of agreement that makes them receptive when you present your offer. Use questions as subheads and paragraph openers.

TEMPLATE:
```
"Have you noticed [obvious thing]? → Yes
Doesn't it drive you crazy when [shared frustration]? → Yes
Wouldn't it be great if [desired outcome]? → Yes
What if I told you [your solution]?"
```

**Double Readership Path:**
- IMPULSIVE readers: skim headlines, subheads, bold text, captions, highlighted sections. This path alone must convey the core offer and benefits.
- ANALYTICAL readers: read everything. Give them facts, statistics, case histories, detailed explanations.
- Design for both. The impulsive path = signposts along the analytical path.

**Internal Repetition — convey your core message 7 ways:**
1. Straightforward statement
2. Example
3. Story / "slice of life"
4. Testimonial
5. Quote from customer/expert
6. Numbered summary

**Page-end teasers:** Never end a page with a completed sentence. Always break mid-sentence to pull reader to next page. Add teaser blurbs: "The 7 secrets revealed on the next page!"

#### Step 11: Rewrite for Style
- Conversational > grammatical. Choppy sentences. Nonsentences. Buzzwords.
- First paragraph = extended headline. Sell the reader on reading the letter.
- Five-senses word pictures. Charged phrases: "Serious as cancer," "So powerful it should be illegal"
- Always incorporate stories — a good story is more persuasive than any list of features
- Let your personality come through. The best letters have a recognizable human voice.

### PHASE 5: Objections & Urgency (Steps 12-14)

#### Step 12: Answer Every Objection
APPLY WHEN: Always. Unanswered questions kill sales letters.

TEMPLATE — The Brainstorm Pattern:
```
"Our team brainstormed every reason you might say no.
After [X] hours, we could only think of [3] possible reasons:
1. [Objection] → [Answer + testimonial + guarantee reference]
2. [Objection] → [Answer + testimonial + guarantee reference]
3. [Objection] → [Answer + testimonial + guarantee reference]"
```

Alternative: FAQ section with objections disguised as questions.

#### Step 13: Spark Immediate Action
Six urgency drivers:
1. **Limited availability** — "When supply is exhausted, your check will be returned uncashed"
2. **Premiums** — Free bonuses for acting now (can boost response 50%+)
3. **Deadlines** — Handwritten/rubber-stamped dates outperform printed ones
4. **Multiple premiums** — If one works, test two
5. **Escalating price** — "Before [date]: $149. After [date]: $199. At the door: $229"
6. **Ease of responding** — Remove every friction point

#### Step 14: The Creative P.S.
APPLY WHEN: Every single sales letter. No exceptions. Many readers skip to the end first — P.S. is the second most-read element after the headline. Use it to summarize the offer, restate the deadline, add a final bonus, or reinforce the guarantee.

---

### PHASE 6-7: Polish, Test & Launch (Steps 15-29)

Steps 15-29 collapsed — production/mechanical: Check against checklist. Apply graphic enhancement (bold, highlights, subheads). Rewrite for passion. Compare to proven examples. Read aloud. Test on real prospects. Cool off 3-5 days. Get second opinions. Split-test variables. "Cut out the parts the reader tends to skip." (Elmore Leonard)

## INTIMIDATION & TAKEAWAY SELLING

Five applications:
1. **Limited number** — "Only X available"
2. **Most will buy** — Bandwagon effect ("Thousands have joined in the last 30 days")
3. **You will buy only if** — Challenge ego ("Only 5 out of 100 respond")
4. **You CAN buy only if** — Application/qualification process
5. **Only some qualify** — Elite/exclusive positioning

---

## GUARANTEE HIERARCHY (Weakest to Strongest)

1. Basic money-back guarantee
2. Refund AND keep the bonus
3. Redundant guarantee ("full 100% refund of every penny")
4. Free trial framing
5. Guarantee as the primary offer focus
6. **Guarantee the letter itself** — "If you read this and feel your time was wasted, I'll pay you $50"

---

## QUICK-REFERENCE CHECKLIST

Before sending any sales message, verify:
- [ ] Written to the reader's priorities, not yours
- [ ] Features translated to benefits (including hidden benefit)
- [ ] Damaging admission included where appropriate
- [ ] Strong headline with flag if applicable
- [ ] P-A-S, Fortune-Telling, or Winners/Losers formula used
- [ ] Price minimized or justified
- [ ] Every objection raised and answered
- [ ] Urgency mechanism in place
- [ ] Strong guarantee
- [ ] Creative P.S.
- [ ] Double readership path (skimmable AND deep)
- [ ] Internal repetition (core message stated 5-7 ways)
- [ ] Yes sequence built into flow
- [ ] Conversational, personality-driven voice
- [ ] Read aloud — flows naturally
