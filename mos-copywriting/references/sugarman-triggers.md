Source: The Adweek Copywriting Handbook, Chapter 19 (Joseph Sugarman)

## All 31 Triggers — Full Reference

### 1. Feeling of Involvement or Ownership
Take the reader on a mental journey of using the product. "Hold it in your hand. See how easily the keys snap to the touch."
- BEST FOR: Product descriptions, landing pages, VSLs
- APPLY WHEN: You need the reader to feel ownership before buying

### 2. Honesty
Include negatives, point out flaws upfront — then explain why they don't matter. Consumers sense dishonesty instantly.
- BEST FOR: All copy — especially high-ticket where trust is critical
- APPLY WHEN: Your product has an obvious weakness. Address it first.

### 3. Integrity
Reflected in ad design, copy tone, overall presentation. Clean and neat signals integrity. Screaming colors and exaggerated claims signal the opposite.
- BEST FOR: Brand copy, about pages, email sequences
- APPLY WHEN: Building long-term brand trust

### 4. Credibility
Being believable. Resolve all objections, explain low prices, piggyback onto credible platforms. Company reputation matters.
- BEST FOR: Sales letters, landing pages, testimonial sections
- APPLY WHEN: Making claims that might seem too good to be true

### 5. Value and Proof of Value
Show through comparison that the purchase is a good deal. Educating the reader on intrinsic value is equivalent to lowering the price.
- BEST FOR: Pricing sections, value stacks, comparison pages
- APPLY WHEN: Price is a potential objection

### 6. Justify the Purchase
Resolve "Can I really justify this?" with savings, health reasons, recognition, "You deserve it." Higher price = more justification needed.
- BEST FOR: Close sections, email sequences, retargeting
- APPLY WHEN: After presenting the price — give them ammunition to tell their spouse

### 7. Greed
Attraction to bargains is powerful. Too low damages credibility unless justified.
- BEST FOR: Discount offers, flash sales, bundle pricing
- APPLY WHEN: Price is genuinely good — but justify WHY it's so low

### 8. Establish Authority
Find something that establishes your expertise. "America's largest..." or even "The hardest-working team in..."
- BEST FOR: About pages, author bios, email signatures, ad copy
- APPLY WHEN: Prospect doesn't know who you are yet

### 9. Satisfaction Conviction
Goes beyond a trial period. Makes them think "They must really believe in their product."
- BEST FOR: Guarantee sections, close copy, checkout pages
- APPLY WHEN: Writing guarantees — make them feel almost too generous

### 10. Nature of Product
Every product has a unique personality. A toy = fun. A medical device = serious. Find the nature and match your tone.
- BEST FOR: Concept development, tone setting
- APPLY WHEN: Starting any new copy project — ask "What is this product's personality?"

### 11. Prospect Nature
Understand the emotional needs driving your prospect. What do they REALLY want?
- BEST FOR: Avatar research, headline creation, emotional hooks
- APPLY WHEN: Before writing — map the emotional landscape

### 12. Current Fads
Recognize trends and harmonize with them — but time it right. Get in early, get out after peak.
- BEST FOR: Social posts, ads, topical content
- APPLY WHEN: A relevant trend aligns with your product

### 13. Timing
Nobody knows if it's the right time — that's why you test. External events change everything overnight.
- BEST FOR: Launch decisions, campaign planning
- APPLY WHEN: Deciding when to run campaigns — test, don't guess

### 14. Linking
Relate your product to something the consumer already knows. "The Nose" smoke detector. "Pocket CB." "Vitamins for your car."
- BEST FOR: Product naming, concept development, headlines
- APPLY WHEN: Introducing a new or complex product — find the familiar bridge

### 15. Consistency
Once committed to a purchase, people buy more to stay consistent. The car salesperson adds floor mats after paperwork is signed.
- BEST FOR: Upsells, order bumps, cross-sells, subscription renewals
- APPLY WHEN: Post-purchase — they've already said yes once

### 16. Harmonize
Match what the market wants first. Meet their needs, then pursue your creative vision.
- BEST FOR: Market positioning, offer design
- APPLY WHEN: Entering a market — study what's already working

### 17. Desire to Belong
People buy to identify with the group that owns that product. Mercedes = Mercedes owners club.
- BEST FOR: Community offers, membership copy, brand identity
- APPLY WHEN: Your product has an aspirational user group

### 18. Desire to Collect
Your best customer for another watch is a watch buyer. People naturally collect.
- BEST FOR: Product lines, limited editions, subscription boxes
- APPLY WHEN: You have multiple products in a category

### 19. Curiosity
The #1 factor in direct marketing success. Don't show too much — let curiosity fill the gap.
- BEST FOR: Headlines, email subject lines, ad hooks, VSL openings
- APPLY WHEN: Always. Withhold just enough to compel the click/read/watch.

### 20. Sense of Urgency
Without it, they say "let me think about it" and never buy. Must be truthful.
- BEST FOR: CTAs, close sections, email sequences, cart abandonment
- APPLY WHEN: Closing any piece of copy. Techniques: limited spots, price increases, deadlines.

### 21. Fear
Loss of opportunity, crime, aging, financial loss. Powerful but use carefully.
- BEST FOR: Problem-aware copy, insurance, security, health
- APPLY WHEN: The consequence of NOT acting is real and significant

### 22. Instant Gratification
Ship fast. Deliver value immediately. Reduce wait time.
- BEST FOR: Checkout pages, delivery promises, digital products
- APPLY WHEN: Competing with alternatives that deliver faster

### 23. Exclusivity, Rarity, Uniqueness
Limited editions, only 6 in the state, members-only. Makes people feel special.
- BEST FOR: Premium offers, waitlists, early access, limited editions
- APPLY WHEN: You can genuinely limit supply or access

### 24. Simplicity
One product, one offer, no choices. Swiss Army Watch test: single watch ad outpulled all-9-models ad 3:1.
- BEST FOR: Offer design, landing pages, ad creative
- APPLY WHEN: Always. Reduce choices. Simplify the path to purchase.

### 25. Human Relationships
Use stories, bylines, first person, humor. Human hands holding products. Create resonance.
- BEST FOR: Brand copy, about pages, social posts
- APPLY WHEN: Copy feels corporate or distant — add the human element

### 26. Storytelling
Stories create emotional bonds. A picture is worth 1,000 words; a story can be invaluable.
- BEST FOR: Email sequences, landing pages, VSLs, social posts
- APPLY WHEN: You have a genuine origin story, customer story, or founder story

### 27. Mental Engagement
Don't say too much — let the reader's mind fill in the gaps. The mind engages more when it draws its own conclusions.
- BEST FOR: Headlines, social posts, ad hooks
- APPLY WHEN: You're over-explaining. Pull back and let them connect the dots.

### 28. Guilt
Making someone feel guilty for NOT buying — through overwhelming value and thorough justification.
- BEST FOR: Value stacks, charity/cause marketing, limited-time offers
- APPLY WHEN: Your value proposition is genuinely strong — "How can you NOT take this?"

### 29. Specificity
"275,683 customers" is more believable than "over 250,000 customers." Specific = credible.
- BEST FOR: Statistics, testimonials, case studies, proof sections
- APPLY WHEN: Any claim you make. Be precise. Precise = honest.

### 30. Familiarity
People buy from people and companies they know. Brand recognition through consistent presence.
- BEST FOR: Retargeting, email sequences, content marketing
- APPLY WHEN: Building awareness over time. Show up consistently.

### 31. Hope
Products offering hope for better health, appearance, wealth, or lifestyle tap deep desire.
- BEST FOR: Health, fitness, wealth, self-improvement copy
- APPLY WHEN: Your product promises a better future state
