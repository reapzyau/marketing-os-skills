# Kennedy Sequence Strategy — Multi-Touch Follow-Up Framework

Source: Dan Kennedy, *The Ultimate Sales Letter* (Chapter 12: "The Million-Dollar Sales Letter Secret")
Purpose: When and how to use multi-step sequences instead of one-shot messages.

---

## Core Principle

"One of the biggest mistakes most marketers make is doing one-shot mailings."

Kennedy calls sequences his "million-dollar secret" — the single idea most responsible for making him and his clients rich. He modeled it after collection agency dunning letters (first notice, second notice, third notice) and applied it to sales.

**The math:** Response from the second and third touches combined will often DOUBLE the response from the first touch alone. Sometimes better.

---

## When to Use Multi-Touch vs Single Piece

**Use a single piece when:**
- One-time promotion or event with a hard deadline
- Existing customers who already trust you and just need the offer
- Very low-cost offer with impulse-buy potential
- You genuinely can't afford to send more

**Use a multi-touch sequence when:**
- Cold prospects who don't know you yet
- High-value offers where one sale covers the cost of the full sequence
- Post-visit / post-demo follow-up (people who showed interest but didn't buy)
- Ongoing lead nurture for an evergreen offer
- Re-activation of lapsed customers
- Any situation where "no" really means "not yet"

---

## Kennedy's Sequence Framework

**Timing:** Tight, compressed — 45 to 60 days total. NOT the slow drip of "brand awareness" over months. Fast, concentrated repetition that creates recognition AND urgency simultaneously.

**Tone escalation:** Modeled on collection letters. Each touch increases urgency, changes the angle, and gives a new reason to respond NOW.

**Key insight:** In any household or office receiving the full sequence, your offer WILL become a topic of conversation. That alone is worth the investment.

---

## The Giorgio's Model (Kennedy's Famous 3-Letter Restaurant Sequence)

Kennedy's most-taught example — used in seminars for 20+ years, shown to 4M+ people. Originally sold inside his $399 Magnetic Marketing System.

**Letter 1 — The Introduction**
- Warm, friendly, introduces the offer
- Makes the core pitch with full benefits
- Includes an incentive to respond
- Tone: "Here's something great for you"

**Letter 2 — The Follow-Up / Reminder**
- References Letter 1: "I wrote to you recently..."
- Adds a new angle or additional proof
- Increases urgency slightly
- Tone: "I'm surprised you haven't responded yet — let me explain why this matters"

**Letter 3 — The Final Notice**
- References both previous letters
- Maximum urgency — deadline, scarcity, or "last chance"
- Often uses humor, guilt, or dramatic escalation
- Tone: "This is your final opportunity — I can't keep this open"

---

## Sequence Applications by Situation

**Post-Visit / No-Sale Follow-Up:**
Kennedy reports converting 10-30% of prospects who initially said no, using follow-up letter sequences within days to weeks. Most businesses wrongly believe "there are no Be-Backs."

**Customer Reactivation:**
Send to lapsed customers. They already know, like, and trust you — they just drifted. A sequence reminds them why they bought in the first place.

**Post-Purchase Reassurance:**
A congratulatory letter sent the day after purchase reduces refunds and buyer's remorse. Reinforces the buying decision.

**New Product to Existing Customers:**
If a customer bought Product A but never Product B, send a sequence about Product B. Kennedy found these consistently pull 2-3% order rates and average $1 gross profit per letter mailed.

## Digital Adaptation Principles

Kennedy's direct mail principles map directly to email, DM, and retargeting sequences:

| Direct Mail Element | Digital Equivalent |
|---|---|
| Envelope face / teaser copy | Email subject line / preview text |
| First-class stamp | Deliverability (warm domain, clean list) |
| Handwritten address | Personalized sender name, no-reply avoidance |
| Grabber / lumpy mail | GIF, video thumbnail, pattern-interrupt element |
| 3-letter sequence over 45-60 days | 5-7 emails over 14-21 days (digital is faster) |
| P.S. | P.S. in email (still the most-read section) |
| Reply card / toll-free number | CTA button + reply option |

**Key digital adjustments:**
- Email sequences compress the timeline (days, not weeks between touches)
- Each email can be shorter — but the SEQUENCE does the full selling job
- Online, you also lose readers to clicks/tabs — urgency matters even more
- Benefits must appear "above the fold" (visible without scrolling)
- Remove every distraction from the conversion path

## 7-Email Sequence Skeleton

Adapt to any offer. Compress or expand timing based on urgency and price point.

### Email 1: The Conversation Opener (Day 0)
**PURPOSE:** Enter their world. Collier Principle — join the conversation already in their head.
- Lead with their #1 pain or desire (from Kennedy's 10 Questions)
- Establish credibility briefly (damaging admission works great here)
- Introduce your solution at a high level
- Soft CTA: learn more / read this / watch this

### Email 2: The Proof (Day 2-3)
**PURPOSE:** Show it works. Build belief.
- Case study, testimonial, or personal story
- Demonstrate ROI or concrete results
- Address the "sounds too good to be true" objection
- CTA: same offer link

### Email 3: The Hidden Benefit (Day 5-6)
**PURPOSE:** Reveal something they hadn't considered.
- P-A-S structure on a secondary pain point
- Show the unexpected benefit (Kennedy's "Hidden Benefit")
- New angle they haven't seen in Emails 1-2
- CTA: same offer link

### Email 4: The Bugaboo (Day 8-9)
**PURPOSE:** Name the enemy. Create us-vs-them solidarity.
- Identify the external villain responsible for their pain
- Channel their anger/frustration toward action
- Position yourself as their ally in the fight
- CTA: "Join us" / "Fight back" framing

### Email 5: The Objection Killer (Day 11-12)
**PURPOSE:** Destroy remaining reasons NOT to buy.
- "I know what you're thinking..." pattern
- Raise and answer 2-3 biggest objections
- Feature the guarantee prominently
- Testimonials that specifically counter objections
- CTA: clear and direct

### Email 6: The Winners & Losers (Day 14-15)
**PURPOSE:** Fear of missing out. Social proof at scale.
- "Two types of [people like you]..." framework
- Show what buyers/members/users are now experiencing
- Contrast with what happens if they do nothing
- Urgency: deadline approaching or spots filling
- CTA: "Don't be the one who..."

### Email 7: The Final Notice (Day 17-21)
**PURPOSE:** Last chance. Maximum urgency.
- Reference the full sequence: "I've written to you X times..."
- Deadline is real and imminent
- Summary of everything included (stack the value)
- Strongest guarantee statement
- Emotional close: what their life looks like after they act
- Final P.S.: restate the deadline and one key benefit

---

## Sequence Rules

1. **Each email must stand alone.** Not everyone opens every email. Each one should make sense independently.
2. **Each email needs a NEW angle.** Don't just repeat — reframe. Different formula, different proof, different emotional trigger.
3. **Urgency must escalate.** Email 1 is warm. Email 7 is "last chance, door closing."
4. **Style can separate from structure.** The Giorgio's structure (intro → reminder → final notice) works whether you're selling pizza or enterprise software. Adjust tone, keep the framework.
5. **Track what works.** Kennedy's split-testing philosophy applies to sequences too — test subject lines, send times, number of emails, and spacing.
