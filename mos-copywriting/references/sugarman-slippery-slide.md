# Sugarman — The Slippery Slide Method

Source: The Adweek Copywriting Handbook (Joseph Sugarman)

---

## The Slippery Slide Formula

Picture a steep slide coated in baby oil. Once the reader starts, they cannot stop.

1. **Compelling headline** → forces reading subheadline
2. **Compelling subheadline** → forces reading first sentence
3. **Very short first sentence** → forces reading second sentence
4. **Each sentence compels the next** → reader cannot stop
5. **Seeds of curiosity** maintain momentum at slow points
6. **If reader gets 25%+ through** → high probability of reading all

APPLY WHEN: Every piece of copy. This is the foundational writing method.

## The 13 Axioms

### Axiom 1: Copywriting Is Mental Processing
Copywriting is a mental process reflecting the sum of all your experiences, specific knowledge, and ability to process that information onto paper for selling.

APPLY WHEN: Preparing to write. Gather deep product knowledge and customer understanding first.

### Axiom 2: Every Element Serves One Purpose
All elements in an ad are primarily designed to do one thing: get you to read the first sentence.

APPLY WHEN: Designing layouts, choosing images, writing headlines. Ask: "Does this make them read the first sentence?"

### Axiom 3: First Sentence → Second Sentence
The sole purpose of the first sentence is to get you to read the second sentence.

APPLY WHEN: Writing openings. Make first sentences SHORT. Examples: "Losing weight is not easy." / "It's you against a computer." / "It had to happen."

### Axiom 4: Create the Buying Environment
Your layout and first few paragraphs must create the buying environment most conducive to the sale.

APPLY WHEN: Setting tone. Discount product → busy graphics, big type. Premium product → clean, refined, trustworthy.

### Axiom 5: Get the Reader Saying Yes
Get the reader to say yes and harmonize with your accurate and truthful statements.

APPLY WHEN: Writing opening paragraphs. Start with statements nobody can disagree with. The moment they say "No" or "I don't believe that" — you've lost them.

### Axiom 6: The Slippery Slide
Readers should be so compelled they cannot stop reading until they read all of it.

APPLY WHEN: Editing. Read your copy aloud. Any place you slow down or want to skip = slide needs more oil there.

### Axiom 7: Don't Assume Constraints
When solving problems, don't assume constraints that aren't really there.

APPLY WHEN: Brainstorming concepts. "You can't sell X through Y" — test it. Every industry-breaking success came from breaking assumed constraints.

### Axiom 8: Curiosity Is Fuel
Keep the copy interesting through the power of curiosity.

APPLY WHEN: Mid-copy momentum drops. Plant Seeds of Curiosity (see below).

### Axiom 9: Sell the Concept, Not the Product
Never sell a product or service. Always sell a concept.

APPLY WHEN: Finding your angle. The concept = positioning, Big Idea, USP. "Pocket CB" sold a concept. "Soviet Challenge" sold a concept. The product is secondary.

### Axiom 10: The Incubation Process
Your subconscious processes information — give it time. Step away after research. The idea will come.

APPLY WHEN: Stuck on a concept. Gather all information, then do something pleasurable. The breakthrough comes during rest.

### Axiom 11: Copy Length = What's Needed
Copy should be long enough to cause the reader to take the action you request. No longer.

APPLY WHEN: Deciding copy length. Higher price + more unusual product = more copy. Known product at low price = less copy.

### Axiom 12: Personal Communication
Every communication should be personal — from the writer to the recipient.

APPLY WHEN: All copy. Use "I" and "you." Write to one person. Use a byline.

### Axiom 13: Logical Flow
Ideas should flow logically, anticipating and answering questions as if asked face-to-face.

APPLY WHEN: Structuring copy. Write each sentence, then ask "What question does the reader have now?" Answer it in the next sentence.

## Seeds of Curiosity

Short phrases at paragraph ends that force reading the next paragraph:

- "But there's more."
- "So read on."
- "But I didn't stop there."
- "Let me explain."
- "Now here comes the good part."
- "But that's not all."
- "Here's why."

APPLY WHEN: Momentum dips in mid-copy. Also use at the opening — mention a benefit you'll reveal later, forcing them to read the whole piece.

## The Copy Sequence (12 Steps)

| # | Element | Purpose |
|---|---------|---------|
| 1 | Headline | Grab attention |
| 2 | Subheadline | Expand + compel to first sentence |
| 3 | Photo/Caption | Illustrate + provide entry point |
| 4 | First sentence | Short, compelling, easy — start the slide |
| 5 | Opening paragraphs | Create buying environment + build harmony |
| 6 | Product story | Features wrapped in emotion + concept |
| 7 | Anticipate objections | Raise them yourself before the reader does |
| 8 | Resolve objections | Every objection raised must be resolved |
| 9 | Justify the purchase | Logic to support the emotional decision |
| 10 | Satisfaction conviction | Go beyond trial period — "They must really believe in this" |
| 11 | Sense of urgency | Truthful reason to buy now |
| 12 | Ask for the order | Always, near the end. Many copywriters forget this |

APPLY WHEN: Structuring any long-form copy (sales letters, landing pages, VSLs, email sequences).

## Seven Steps to Writing Great Copy

1. **Become an expert** on the product/service
2. **Know your prospect** — motivations, profile, what makes them buy
3. **Write headline and subheadline** — short headline, ~16-word subheadline, shortest possible first sentence
4. **Write the copy** — emotional outpouring, don't worry about structure yet
5. **Edit the copy** — spelling, grammar, tighten, eliminate waste
6. **Incubate** — step away, do something pleasurable, come back fresh
7. **Final look** — you'll catch things you missed

## Core Philosophy

- Sell the concept, not the product
- Sell on emotion, justify with logic
- Honesty is the most powerful selling tool
- Every word has an emotion attached to it
- The first draft is always terrible — editing is where the magic happens
- Test everything — you never really know what will work
- The consumer is smarter than you think
