## 0. The One-Sentence Summary

Copywriting is selling *emotions and a desired self*, not products. You do that by researching what a single reader truly wants (in their own words), matching one idea to it, and articulating it through a structure that grabs attention, overcomes objections, and asks for one action. **It's 80% research, 20% writing — and it's a practice skill, not a knowledge skill.**

---

## 1. Foundations (the principles that never expire)

**The three levels of persuasion** — learn the deepest layer or you'll be stranded when tactics saturate:
1. **Tactics** — temporary, easily copied (a button colour that lifts conversions).
2. **Strategies** — durable frameworks (a particular email style for selling dog treats).
3. **Principles** — the psychology of *why* people decide. The only layer that survives as markets evolve.

**Core truths:**
- **People don't buy products. They buy outcomes, emotions, and a desired self.** Sell the identity they'll have after using it.
- **Emotion decides; logic justifies.** Hit the emotional core *before* the logical brain intervenes. People feel first, then look for reasons.
- **You have 1–2 seconds to engage.** Every word's job is to earn the next word, until the reader has "bought in just a little bit."
- **A confused mind does not buy.** Clarity beats cleverness every time.
- **Novelty is non-negotiable.** If the reader can categorise your copy as something they've seen before, they stop reading. (The "New Test": ask *will they file this under something familiar?* If yes, it fails.)
- **Don't make copy draw attention to itself** — it should be a "gossamer veil" the reader sees *through* to the thing they want.
- **Two most powerful words:** *you* (#1) and *because* (#2). Always give a reason-why for any claim, ask, or discount — even a weak reason lifts compliance (Cialdini).

**Two kinds of copy:**
- **Direct response** — get action *now* (the focus of this playbook).
- **Brand / delayed response** — build affinity over time.

## 2. Diagnose the Reader: Awareness & Sophistication (Eugene Schwartz)

Everything — what you say, the order you say it, where you start — is determined by these two dials. Get them wrong and nothing sells.

### 2.1 The Five Stages of Awareness

| Stage | They know… | Where to start the copy |
|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | nothing — no problem, no desire | Don't mention price/product/what it does. Find *anything* they care about; each sentence's only job is to pull them to the next. (→ long-form sales letters, VSLs, welcome sequences) |
| **Problem Aware** | they have a problem, but not that solutions exist | Show you feel their pain, build trust first, then reveal a "hidden solution." Target the worry that keeps them up at night. |
| **Solution Aware** | a solution type exists, not *your* product | Call out the desire/problem, prove your product delivers, show the mechanism. Indirect/tempered appeals last longer than gimmicks. |
| **Product Aware** | your product, but haven't committed | Prove claims, back promises. Use one of the 7 product-aware hooks (§5.4). |
| **Most Aware** | the product, want it, need a reason to act now | Direct offer, discount, deadline, announcement + a believable reason-why. |

**Diagnostic shortcut — answer in order:**
1. Do they know they have a problem/desire? *No → Unaware.*
2. Do they know a solution exists? *No → Problem Aware.*
3. Do they know your product (or close competitors)? *No → Solution Aware.*
4. Do they know the price / are they hunting for a reason to buy? *Yes → Most Aware.*
> It's a *tool, not a jewel* — take your best guess and keep moving. In long copy you walk the reader up through the stages within a single piece.

### 2.2 The Five Stages of Market Sophistication

How tired the market is of your *type* of claim:
1. **Stage 1** — first to market. A big, plain promise works ("Make money online").
2. **Stage 2** — still early. Same promise, slightly louder.
3. **Stage 3** — copied. Make the promise **bigger or more specific** ("$2,500–$10,000 every Thursday").
4. **Stage 4** — every extreme promise has been made. Introduce a **new mechanism** — a new *way* the old promise works.
5. **Stage 5** — the mechanism is copied too. Shift to **indirect appeals** to notions, identifications, and characteristics. The *idea* becomes the differentiator.

## 3. The Rule of One — RIOA

Define exactly one of each **before writing a single word**. *"If you try to appeal to everybody, you persuade nobody."*

- **R — One Reader.** A single avatar: their desires, notions, identifications, characteristics, where they hang out online, stage of awareness, stage of sophistication. (Get this from the client — "if you targeted one person, who?" — or from your own research.)
- **I — One Idea.** The single most powerful point connecting reader → product. Three starter angles:
  1. Big relevant benefit + how it works (low-sophistication markets).
  2. The unique *mechanism* that delivers the benefit (sophisticated markets).
  3. Why believe you over competitors (the USP).
- **O — One Offer** (or **Objective** if non-transactional). An offer is *not a product* — it's "a web of promises and benefits and bonuses": deliverable + components that prove the benefit + guarantees + bonuses + price + delivery speed/access.
- **A — One Action.** One specific, descriptive ask ("click to see if it's right for your dog," not "submit"). Fewer asks = less decision paralysis = more conversion.

### 3.1 The Reader's Emotional Core — DNIC
Every decision is driven by four areas. Research them, write to them:
- **Desires** — goals they want / pains they want gone (superficial *and* deep).
- **Notions** — beliefs about the world.
  - *Firm notions* = held dear, can't be shifted (e.g. "palm oil harms animals"). **Violate one and you lose the reader instantly.**
  - *Shakable notions* = beliefs they'd happily be wrong about ("learning guitar takes ages"). These are your **best objections** — acknowledge and overturn them.
- **Identifications** — chosen labels and affiliations (sports teams, "vegan," "MMA fighter").
- **Characteristics** — unchosen traits (age, income, body). Use sparingly, only when an obvious appeal presents itself.

## 4. Research — where 80% of the work lives

> *"If you research well and follow the research, you can write good copy without any formula."*

### 4.1 The Sticky Research Process (full phase)
1. **Product research** — list *every* feature → translate each to a benefit → drill to deeper benefits. Find USPs, brand image, authority points (certifications, user counts, endorsements).
2. **Competitor research** — what messages are they running? Check Facebook Ad Library, Google Ad Transparency, TikTok Ad Library. Join their email lists. Buy their products.
3. **Market research (IVOC)** — collect indirect voice-of-customer data (below).
4. **Fill the one-reader profile** from the IVOC data.
5. **Form the One Idea.**
6. **Define Offer & Action (RIOA).**

**Research-to-fee ratio:** Full campaign (sales letter + emails + order form + upsells) ≈ 4–5 wks research, 1–2 wks writing, 1 wk revision/compliance. A single $-cheap value email? "Google two things." Scale effort to the fee.

### 4.2 IVOC — Indirect Voice Of Customer
**Never ask people what they want directly.** Ogilvy: *"People don't think what they feel, don't say what they think, and don't do what they say."* Surveys and focus groups give socially-filtered answers (Red Bull ignored awful taste tests and won; Ouya had glowing surveys and died). Go where prospects speak *unguarded*.

**Six buckets:** Desires · Firm notions · Shakable notions · Identifications · Characteristics · Other.

**Process:**
1. Search where they hang out: Reddit, YouTube comments, Facebook groups, forums, Amazon reviews, Quora. (*Hack:* append "forum" to any Google search. *Tool:* Keywords Everywhere to see exact search phrases — e.g. discover prospects search "arthritis" not "joint pain," and rewrite to match.)
2. Copy **exact quotes** in *italics* (preserve their language).
3. Note recurring words/phrases.
4. Write **IVOC summaries** — condense related quotes into one plain sentence.
5. **Tally frequency** of each summary.
6. **Rank by frequency** → this becomes your messaging priority order.

**Stop rule:** ~10 new sources with no new data points = done. The point is *immersion* (vocabulary, nuance, emotional triggers), not the document.

**AI rule:** Use AI only to *find research locations* ("list 10 forums where senior-dog owners discuss problems"). Never to do the research or write the copy — it only recycles existing copy, can't tell good from bad, and produces generic output. *"If you rely on AI to write or research your copy, you don't have the skill yourself."*

### 4.3 Translate research into copy (the IVOC table)
Three columns: **spit-draft point → raw IVOC quote → finished copy line.** Pull the metaphors/adjectives from real quotes and merge.
> *Raw:* "I don't want to be a picky food princess and force my friends to change their plans" → *Copy:* "Forcing friends and family to make last-minute menu changes isn't fun."
> *Raw:* "got some of her bounciness back" → *Copy:* "Get their bounciness back."

## 5. The Message: Features → Benefits → Deeper Benefits, Promises & Hooks

### 5.1 FABD ladder
- **Feature** — objective spec ("lasts 24 hours").
- **Advantage** — what it does *better than alternatives* ("cures warts faster than other brands").
- **Benefit** — the positive outcome the customer experiences ("no charger to carry").
- **Deeper benefit** — the unconscious *why behind the why*; who they become (the desired self).

**Drill tool — "But what does that really mean?"** Ask it (or "why?") repeatedly until you hit a line that makes you go *"oh, damn."* That's the deeper benefit.
> *Dan Kennedy hearing-aids:* customers weren't buying "hear the birds again" — they feared being seen as demented and put in a home. Entirely different copy.
> *Sean McIntyre financial:* connects wealth → altruism to dissolve the buyer's guilt about wanting money.

**When to go deep vs. stay on features:** Feature-led works when people already want the thing (chocolate, PCs, Lamborghinis). Go to benefits/deeper benefits the moment you're **asking for money/commitment**, selling against alternatives, or fighting for attention in a sophisticated market. Don't *invent* deeper benefits — infer from evidence; when unsure, test.

### 5.2 The Promise (the most basic tool)
A statement — explicit or implied — that the copy can fulfil the reader's desire.
- *Explicit:* "Turn your business into a self-scaling company that drives itself to eight figures."
- *Implicit:* "Finally, win the battle with her waistline." (never says "weight loss" — the subconscious connects it)

**A good promise is:** benefit-driven · clear ("a confused mind doesn't buy") · believable/provable · cohesive with the CTA. **Bigger claim = more proof required.**

### 5.3 The Hook (the single most important element)
Ogilvy: ~80% of success is the headline. The hook is the first point of contact — headline, subject line, thumbnail, first 3 seconds of a video. You can't sell to someone who isn't paying attention.

Two idea-template hooks:
- **Low sophistication:** *"Here's the big relevant benefit + this is how you get it."*
- **Sophisticated / USP:** *"This method you've never seen is the key to [desire] — and it's only attainable through [product]."* (Optionally layer a shakable notion: "easier than you think.")

### 5.4 Seven ways to hook a Product-Aware audience
1. Strengthen desire via someone they admire using it (Air Jordans).
2. Demonstrate it meets their need (Rolls-Royce: *"At 60mph the loudest noise is the electric clock"*).
3. Highlight situations where it shines.
4. New proof / detail / evidence.
5. Announce a new mechanism.
6. Reveal updates that fix old limitations.
7. Reposition entirely against competitors.

### 5.5 Tools that sharpen the message
- **Positioning** — which details you foreground, in what order, for which audience. Same product, different positioning = different ad (Iron Neck: injury-prevention for MMA; tech-neck/posture for gamers). *"Wheat fired from guns"* and *"It's toasted"* — production facts every competitor shared, but only one mentioned.
- **Mimicking** — use the audience's exact jargon ("MVP" to sports fans) → intimacy and trust.
- **Dimensionalization** — recast a claim to add a dimension (emotional/historical/sensory) or move it up the FABD ladder. "46% off" → "virtually half the price."
- **Gimmicks** — secondary reasons to act (urgency, scarcity, clickbait, secrets, bonuses, deadlines). Always pair with a **reason-why**.
- **Asyndeton** — drop the "and," use a comma: "the stylish, comfortable design" (flows better, avoids "and…and…and").
- **The Game of Promises** — practice writing different promises for the same product to different readers; forces you to find each segment's emotional core.

## 6. Structure

### 6.1 Hook / Body / Close (the universal architecture)
- **Hook** — grab attention.
- **Body** — channel it toward the action; **overcome objections** (more sophistication/skepticism = more proof).
- **Close** — give them the chance to act (CTA).
> No correct length. All three can live in one sentence or across a 20-page letter — depends how much convincing is needed. One short ad: *"86% off noise-cancelling headphones — Sony, Bose, Beats — today only"* (hook = offer, body = brand proof, close = "today only").

### 6.2 Body copy: OCPB
- **O — Objection** (call it out directly → validates the reader).
- **C — Claim** (the contrary claim, no cushioning: "Sales copy has to be tight, tight, tight").
- **P — Proof** (dates, numbers, named sources, past predictions, third-party citations).
- **B — Benefit** (remind them what they gain by believing you).
For skeptical/cold audiences stack it: **O → C → P → P → P → B.**

**Selling against alternatives:** instead of defending your product, attack the credibility of competing solutions ("you'll never make money listening to [financial media] — they only cover what's already big").

### 6.3 The Five Subconscious Questions
The reader is silently asking these at all times — answer all five somewhere:
1. How is this relevant to me *right now*?
2. Will this be *new* to me?
3. Do I understand the *benefits* of acting?
4. Do I understand *how* those benefits are delivered?
5. Do I *trust* whoever's talking?

### 6.4 DIC (short copy: lift emails, FB ads)
- **D — Disrupt** (stop the scroll).
- **I — Intrigue** (build curiosity with specific detail; tease, don't tell).
- **C — Click** (one CTA).
> The course's claim: DIC *subsumes* AIDA / PAS / PASTOR because it's less prescriptive — it doesn't force you to open on a fixed element. **Principles over formulas:** prescriptive templates are *"a stupid way of writing"* — plot to the reader's needs instead.

## 7. The Writing Process — Action Alchemy Ritual (SEAR)

An 8-step path from blank page to finished copy:
1. **Lay out RIOA.**
2. **Check context** — format requirements + message-matching (what did they see *before* this, what comes *after*?).
3. **Answer the Five Subconscious Questions.**
4. **Compile objections** — product/audience-specific, beyond the five.
5. **Plot hook, body, close.**
6. **Spit draft** — describe *what* you'll say and *in what order* (not the copy itself). Rank points by IVOC frequency. e.g. "State the one idea + their problem can be fixed today" / "Attack solutions that failed them" / "Reveal the real cause."
7. **Translate IVOC** — map raw quotes to spit-draft points.
8. **Write** — execute from the plan.

**Drafting discipline:** *"Write everything out of your head first, then cut with a weed-whacker."* Never break flow hunting for the perfect word — drop an `X` and move on. Writing surfaces *new* objections — note and handle them mid-draft.

**Message matching** is a conversion-killer when broken: a price on the sales page that differs from the checkout page breaks the sale. Always check the link before and after.

## 8. Formats

### 8.1 Email — the campaign system
Five email types:
1. **Editorial / Value email** — teach a real lesson + infotainment. Carries an **Edman** (editorial mention: a subtle, embedded, non-pushy link). Soft push only. (Masters: Ben Settle, Tarzan Kay.)
2. **Lift** — short; *only* job is to drive the click to the sales page. **Never recapitulate the sales argument** — it drains the page's persuasive power. DIC structure. Never put the full deal ("85% off") in the subject line — that kills the reason to click.
3. **Sales email** — full sales argument (benefits, proof, objection handling) → order form.
4. **Graphical sales email** — image/GIF + headline + short copy + CTA button. Best for **e-commerce**, not info products. State the best deal succinctly.
5. **Deadline email** — scarcity + urgency, short and firm. No intrigue — action is the only goal.

**Campaign cadence:** ~1 email/day; 2–3 on the final day. Start value-heavy/low-push → crossfade to urgency/deadline as it closes. **Don't send only promo emails** (spam folder) — value emails keep opens healthy.

**Link Sandwich** (value emails): subtle link early · teaser link middle · direct CTA at the end.

**The Welcome Email** (first email after signup):
1. Salutation ("this just happened — you signed up") → 2. Congratulate the decision → 3. Introduce the business/guru (**weave credentials in as benefits**, not a bio) → 4. Necessary info + links → 5. **CTA** (welcome emails are the rare exception where multiple CTAs are OK) → 6. **PS** (secondary offer / whitelist instruction / tease tomorrow's email).
> *"Brian just about wiped out when he got the notification. He's one of the top instructors in the country, top-10 on the Pro Tour for 15 years — he can't wait to see you gliding across the waves."* (feature → woven benefit)

**PS — valid reasons to use one:** (a) a second idea that would break the email's rule-of-one; (b) a critical detail that didn't fit the flow; (c) tie back to a deliberately disconnected subject line; (d) sprinkle urgency/scarcity not in the body.

**Email metrics reality:** open rates are now unreliable (Apple/Android proxies pre-load and register false opens). **Clicks** are the primary metric, then earnings-per-click, then deliverability. Don't split-test subject lines below tens of thousands of sends. **Unsubscribes are good** — they clean the list and raise engagement. Best email windows: pre-work morning, lunch, 3–4pm, 9–10pm. Always make images clickable.

### 8.2 The Sales Page
**Lead type spectrum** (warmer audience / clearer problem = more direct):
- **Indirect lead** → intrigue first, then push to desire.
- **Promise lead** → lead with the big benefit/claim.
- **Offer lead** → present offer + price + action immediately.

**Plot (not a formula):**
1. Call out the one reader + the core idea (hook/promise).
2. Authority / social proof — *brief* (≤3 lines personal cred, then team cred).
3. Back the idea with logic — *why* it's true.
4. **Not Statements** — who this is **not** for (repels wrong buyers, makes the right reader self-qualify): *"This is not for beginners… not for anyone who can't afford an investment in themselves."*
5. **Offer reveal** — early, not buried.
6. Why the product is good (why it was built, what it covers).
7. Module/content breakdown with **fascination bullets**.
8. Behind-the-scenes / transparency moment.
9. **Testimonials** — big push.
10. Final CTA + urgency.

**Sales-page tactics:**
- **Eyebrow copy** — qualifier line above the headline ("For working copywriters who want to break their earning ceiling").
- **Validate the claim before expanding** — make a big claim, then immediately "more on that in a minute" (open loop) + proof.
- **Create an enemy** — set the offer against guru advice / industry assumption ("They're lying to you — the key isn't copywriting").
- **Negative-review judo** — show a 1-star review, then discredit the reviewer (they criticised a promise you never made) → *boosts* trust. "There's a 3.8% chance this won't help you" reads as honesty.
- **Three Options Close** (Brunson): (1) do this [best], (2) inferior path [harder/pricier], (3) do nothing [worst]. "The choice is yours."
- **Price anchoring** — standard (high → your price) or **reverse** (anchor at zero, then add value: "we never include guarantees — but for this you get a full refund").
- **Specificity = credibility** — "104+ business owners," "$6,400/mo → $25,000/mo," never round.
- **"It isn't your fault"** — relieves blame, lowers defences. **Tell people not to buy if they can't afford it** — builds trust, cuts refunds/chargebacks.

## 9. Editing — three passes, in order

1. **Value edits** (first, most important) — *is the idea good enough?* Unique, compelling, genuinely new? If not, stop — **a polished turd is still a turd.**
2. **Functional edits** — does the structure work? Reorder sections; check every section ties to the **golden thread** (core idea); use **reverse outlining** (write what each section *does*) to learn and fix structure.
3. **Line edits** (last, least important) — read **aloud**; kill weak verbs ("helps" → "eliminates/destroys"; "learn" → "discover"; avoid "is/are/was"); vary sentence length (short after long = punch); `Ctrl+F` repeated words ("just," "if") and break the pattern.

> Writing and editing are **different skills** — practise them separately. Don't optimise words; **swap ideas** — 90% of line tweaks won't move results; the quality/emotion of the *idea* does.

## 10. Getting Paid — clients, outreach, pricing, calls

### 10.1 Niching
- **Don't niche at the start** unless you have a real domain reason (6 yrs a vet → vet copy is fine). *"Start wide; your niche will choose you."* Money is everywhere (*"$126,000 from the industrial piping niche"*).
- **Intermittent niching:** pick an interesting niche → confirm businesses make money there → build a mini-portfolio → send 10–30 quality pitches → switch niches and repeat. One niche *per batch*, never a permanent lock-in.

### 10.2 Cold outreach (outreach *is* copy — the product is you)
**Three things must happen:** they (1) pay attention → *be different + relevant*; (2) believe you can help → *demonstrate how + be someone they want to work with*; (3) think value > cost → *minimum viable vision + easy next step*.

- **Quality > quantity:** 20 personalised sends (50% open) beat 200 blasts (10% open). Think personal gift vs. branded keychain.
- **Funnel-hack for spec work:** Google a niche → sign up to a business's list → no welcome email? = opportunity → write a spec welcome email (~20 min). Pull their bio into reader-benefits.
- **Attention tactics:** reference a recent event/award/podcast; reference a hobby; lateral subject lines ("Yep, you ruined my shoes"); very short *or* very long subject lines; be a customer ("your cookie box sold out again — you could do the same for the red velvet"); reference their dog ("Milo looks like a good boy"); loop **two decision-makers** in one thread for internal FOMO.
- **Prove value:** bulleted findings + attached PDF audit; a **Loom video audit** (3–10 min) walking their funnel; a tailored spec piece offered free ("use it — if it works, hire me"); competitor-gap comparison.
- **Present the offer (Minimum Viable Vision):** "I can make these improvements over a two-week period and deliver to your team — happy to discuss on a quick call." Add a timeframe, a guarantee, a bonus, a **diagram** (humans are visual), and an **easy next step** ("Thursday afternoon work?").
- **Follow up ≥7 times**, ~1 week apart, **something new each time** (never "did you see this?"), across different platforms. Assume the *hook* failed, not the value.

### 10.3 Job boards (Upwork/Fiverr) — warm leads
- Profile = the **value to the client**, not your bio. First line: "I do X to get you Y."
- Specific job title ("brand storytelling copywriter," not "copywriter" — platform SEO).
- About: (1) value proposition, (2) background + client types, (3) deliverables, (4) other experience, (5) CTA to message.
- Personalise every proposal; reference the posting; upload relevant samples; **match your rate to the job's budget** (default auto-fills high and disqualifies you). Take small jobs early — credibility snowballs.
- Plain English, **no jargon** (PAS/fascination bullets mean nothing to clients). Sell *value*, never outcomes you can't control. Don't lie.

### 10.4 The CV (it's copy — you're the product)
- Bold header + photo (pattern interrupt + trust). **Intrigue opener / open loop:** *"Want to know why an obscure 1903 newspaper ad holds the secret to influencer marketing? It helped me earn £215,000 for a national supplier. I'll share it by the end of this CV."* → close the loop at the end.
- Mirror the job-ad's language in your skills. Order by **relevance**, not chronology. Logo banner = social proof. One project told as a story with screenshots + metrics.
- *"1–2 pages" is bad advice* — write as much as is relevant (his 12-page CV landed a six-figure job). Never adjective-soup openers ("determined, creative, sociable…"). Customise per role.

### 10.5 Networking (slow money, big money)
Go to **build relationships, not hunt transactions** (transactional intent is detectable and kills trust). Networking bypasses gatekeepers.

**FORD — conversation starters:** **F**amily · **O**ccupation · **R**ecreation · **D**reams (Dreams used mid/late, never as opener). Open-ended only, never yes/no. *"What are you working on right now that's exciting?"* · *"How did you get into [field]?"* · *"What good books have you read lately?"*

- **Recapitulate** their words back ("sounds like X is the real problem") → shows listening, segues to pitch.
- Make them want to give *you* their card (business-card-giving is weak — Mark Ford).
- Compliment **specifics** ("your barber did a great job"), keep a **memory log** of 1–3 details per contact.
- **226 follow-up:** 2 weeks → 2 months → 6 months. Never lead with a favour.
> *DM'd a former colleague (now a copy chief), signed a $20k gig within 20 minutes.*

### 10.6 Pricing
- **Always per project, never per hour.**
- **Pro-rata range:** ideal monthly income (e.g. $6k) and minimum survivable (e.g. $3k) = your range. Estimate the % of working time the project eats; charge that slice. Quote at the **top** of the range, leave room to come down in increments.

### 10.7 The Sales Call — Show-and-Tell (10 steps)
Act like a **doctor** (diagnose → prescribe), never a **waiter** (here's a menu). Only two things *must* happen: explain how you'd help, and state your price.
1. **Real talk** — specific, relevant small talk (not "how are you?").
2. **Set the agenda** — name the 3 parts of the call upfront.
3. **Confirmation questions** — "anything you'd really like to discuss?" / "what made a call with me seem worthwhile?" / "what does success look like in 6 months?"
4. **State your aim** — name 1–3 problems you found (opens loops).
5. **Vision & logic** — share screen, show their *live* assets: problem → why it's a bottleneck → what fixing it means in real terms → the solution (spec/swipe) → the logic → "can you see why that'd help?"
6. **Proof** — past results, spec pieces, or "Company X did this and had their best quarter."
7. **Offer slide** — deliverables, timeframe, relationship type. **No price on the slide.**
8. **Buy-in, then price verbally** — "Does that sound like a good plan?" → wait for yes → state price confidently → "If that feels fair, want to move ahead?" → **say nothing.**
9. **Meta close** (if they hesitate) — "I won't hard-sell. I want to work with you because [specific]. What isn't working about the plan?"
10. **Kill objections:**
    - *"Too expensive"* → "Are you worried you won't make your money back, or is it something else?"
    - *"Need to think"* → "If it were free, would you accept?"
    - *"Want to look around"* → "Worried about working with someone who approached you first? The research I've done is exactly the commitment you won't get elsewhere."
    - *"Need to ask my partner"* → "What do you think their concerns would be?"
    - Repeated no → "How about we don't decide just yet?" + book a follow-up + ask what you could've done better.

**Four sales-call principles:** people buy to cure pains they *already* know they have · they don't buy on uncertainty · they don't hire people they don't like · they hire on expertise / cost-effectiveness / speed / ease.

### 10.8 Referrals
Success = **40% quality of work / ease to work with · 40% timing of the ask · 20% how you ask.** Best time = right at project completion or when they wrap a big win. Be specific: *"I'm looking to connect with high-ticket life coaches — anyone like that in your network?"* not "know anyone who needs a copywriter?"

### 10.9 The real money truth
Copywriting is **not** get-rich-quick — it's the **foundation**. The high-income paths: (1) juggle many freelance clients; (2) reach the top 1–5% at a direct-response publisher with royalties/commissions; (3) **expand beyond copywriting into full marketing strategy** — become the "everything marketer" a business owner can hand any problem to. Path 3 is the most reliable.

## 11. The Practice Regimen

- **Copywriting is a practice skill, not a knowledge skill.** You build the "golden gut" by writing and consuming ads — not by reading about it. Turn off ad-block; *"if I wanted to write short stories, I'd read short stories."*
- **Taking more shots > improving your aim.** Even elite copywriters hit 30–40%. One $40M writer wrote **34 VSLs** before his blockbuster. Volume is the game.
- **The Daily 3:** study one piece of copy (go *outside* your niche), write one page, write down one idea.
- **25 / 50 / 25 (Robert Bly):** 25% learning · 50% the work (write/research/revise) · 25% outreach (do it last — lowest cognitive load).
- **Starter portfolio:** 1 squeeze page · 1 welcome email · 3 sales/lift emails · 1 Facebook ad.
- **Permission to suck:** first ~50 pieces are garbage — normal. Don't chase a critique until *you* can't find one more improvement. Put work away 1–2 weeks before rereading. Don't try to do everything at once (high "affective filter" = paralysis). **Done beats perfect** — a 2-week promo once sold $50M+ and was the control for years.

## 12. Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **DNIC** | Desires, Notions, Identifications, Characteristics — the reader's emotional core. |
| **RIOA** | Rule of One: one Reader, Idea, Offer (or Objective), Action. |
| **FABD** | Feature → Advantage → Benefit → Deeper benefit. |
| **Deeper benefit** | The unconscious "why behind the why"; the desired self. |
| **Desired self** | The identity the reader wants to become — what you're really selling. |
| **State of awareness** | How much the prospect knows: Unaware → Problem → Solution → Product → Most Aware. |
| **Market sophistication** | How tired the market is of your *type* of claim (Stages 1–5). |
| **New mechanism** | A new *way* an old promise works; refreshes belief in a sophisticated market. |
| **Firm / shakable notion** | Beliefs that can't be shifted (don't violate) vs. beliefs they'd happily drop (your best objections). |
| **Promise** | Explicit/implicit statement that the copy will fulfil the desire. |
| **Gimmick** | Secondary reason to act (urgency, scarcity, bonus) — always with a reason-why. |
| **Positioning** | Which product details you foreground, in what order, for whom. |
| **Dimensionalization** | Recasting a claim to add an emotional/sensory/historical dimension. |
| **Mimicking** | Using the audience's exact jargon. |
| **Asyndeton** | Dropping "and" for a comma to improve flow. |
| **Hook / Body / Close** | Grab attention → overcome objections → ask for action. |
| **OCPB** | Objection → Claim → Proof → Benefit (body-copy structure). |
| **DIC** | Disrupt → Intrigue → Click (short copy / lift emails / ads). |
| **Five Subconscious Questions** | Relevant? New? Benefits? How delivered? Trust? |
| **SEAR / Action Alchemy** | The 8-step writing process. |
| **Spit draft** | Plan of *what* you'll say and in what order — not the copy. |
| **IVOC** | Indirect Voice Of Customer — unguarded prospect language from forums/reviews. |
| **IVOC summary** | One plain sentence condensing many related quotes; ranked by frequency. |
| **Golden thread** | The core idea every section must tie back to. |
| **Reverse outlining** | Writing what each section of existing copy *does*, to learn structure. |
| **Value / Functional / Line edits** | Idea quality → structure → sentence polish (in that order). |
| **Unique mechanism / USP** | The named vehicle that delivers the benefit; what only you offer. |
| **Lift email** | Short email whose only job is the click to the sales page. |
| **Edman** | Editorial mention — a subtle product link inside a value email. |
| **Link sandwich** | Subtle / teaser / direct links across a value email. |
| **Not statements** | Copy listing who the offer is *not* for (self-qualifies the right reader). |
| **Eyebrow copy** | Qualifier line above the headline. |
| **Fascination bullets** | Curiosity-building teaser bullets. |
| **Three Options Close** | Do this (best) / inferior path / do nothing (worst). |
| **Price anchoring** | Reference price set before the real price; standard (high→low) or reverse (zero→value). |
| **Message matching** | Congruence across the funnel — what they saw before/after must line up. |
| **Control** | The piece that beats everything tested against it. |
| **Spec piece** | Practice copy for a real business, written unpaid to win the work. |
| **Minimum Viable Vision** | A brief, clear "here's what I'll do, by when, next step" offer. |
| **FORD** | Family / Occupation / Recreation / Dreams — networking conversation starters. |
| **226 follow-up** | Re-contact at 2 weeks, 2 months, 6 months. |
| **Pro-rata pricing** | Charge a slice of your income range based on % of time consumed. |
| **Doctor vs. waiter** | Diagnose & prescribe (right) vs. present a menu (wrong). |
| **Meta close** | Disarm by openly refusing the hard sell, then surface the real objection. |
| **Everything marketer** | A copywriter who can fix any business's marketing — the real high-income path. |
