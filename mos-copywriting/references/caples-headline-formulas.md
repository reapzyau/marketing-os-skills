# Caples Headline Formulas

Source: *Tested Advertising Methods* (5th Ed.) — John Caples, revised by Fred E. Hahn.
Caples' hierarchy: Self-Interest > News > Curiosity. Every headline should contain at least one.

---

## 1. Self-Interest Headlines (promise a direct benefit — Caples' #1 type)
**"How to [achieve desired outcome]"**
Example: "How to Win Friends and Influence People"
BEST FOR: tutorials, courses, guides, email subjects, landing pages
POWER LEVEL: High — Caples called "How to" practically immortal

**"How [story element] [gave me / made me] [result]"**
Example: "How a Fool Stunt Made Me a Star Salesman"
BEST FOR: long-form ads, emails, social posts, case studies
POWER LEVEL: High — combines self-interest + curiosity + story

**"How I [achieved result] in [specific timeframe]"**
Example: "How I Improved My Memory in One Evening"
BEST FOR: landing pages, course promos, lead magnets
POWER LEVEL: High — adds quick/easy proof to self-interest

**"[Number] Ways to [get benefit]"**
Example: "Five New Ways to Say I Love You Every Day"
BEST FOR: blog posts, social, email subjects, listicle content
POWER LEVEL: Medium — reliable but common; specificity helps

**"Give Me [small commitment] and I'll Give You [big promise]"**
Example: "Give Me 5 Days and I'll Give You a Magnetic Personality"
BEST FOR: landing pages, direct response ads, email sequences
POWER LEVEL: High — tested by Caples as one of his top 10 all-time

**"Who Else Wants [desirable outcome]?"**
Example: "Who Else Wants a Whiter Wash — With No Hard Work?"
BEST FOR: social ads, email subjects, community posts
POWER LEVEL: Medium — implies others already benefit; social proof baked in

**"To [specific person] Who Wants [specific result]"**
Example: "To a $25,000 Man or Woman Who Would Like to Be Making $50,000"
BEST FOR: targeted ads, email segmentation, niche landing pages
POWER LEVEL: High — selects audience AND promises benefit in one line

**"[You / You Too] Can [desirable outcome]"**
Example: "You Too Can Beat the Market"
BEST FOR: courses, membership promos, social ads | POWER LEVEL: Medium

**"I Gambled [small thing] and Won [big result]"**
Example: "I Gambled a Postage Stamp and Won $35,840 in 2 Years"
BEST FOR: direct response, email subjects, story-driven ads | POWER LEVEL: High

**"Wanted — Your Services as a [desirable role]"**
Example: "Wanted — Your Services as a High-Paid Real-Estate Specialist"
BEST FOR: recruitment, course ads, landing pages | POWER LEVEL: High

## 2. News Headlines (announce something new — Caples' #2 type)
**"Introducing [new product/service/feature]"**
Example: "Introducing the All-New Ford Taurus"
BEST FOR: product launches, feature announcements, email blasts
POWER LEVEL: High — Caples said to spread "Introducing" in large type

**"Announcing [new thing or improvement]"**
Example: "Announcing a New Course for Men and Women Who Want to Be Independent"
BEST FOR: product launches, community announcements, press
POWER LEVEL: High — one of Caples' top 10 tested headlines

**"At Last — [solution to known problem]"**
Example: "At Last! A Steam Iron with a 'Magic Brain'"
BEST FOR: product launches, landing pages, email subjects
POWER LEVEL: High — implies long-awaited solution; urgency built in

**"Now [previously unavailable thing is available]"**
Example: "Now You Can Learn Both Ways to Write for Publication"
BEST FOR: feature launches, access announcements, course drops | POWER LEVEL: Medium

**"New [specific product/method/approach]"**
Example: "New Method of Keeping Your Personal Finances"
BEST FOR: any launch or update; all channels | POWER LEVEL: Medium

**"[Date/Time] — [specific event or offer]"**
Example: "Beginning June 1 — Low Summer Rates at the Miami Biltmore"
BEST FOR: promotions, events, limited-time offers | POWER LEVEL: Medium

**"Discovered — [breakthrough or method]"**
Example: "Discovered — Amazing Way to Grow Hair"
BEST FOR: direct response, self-improvement, emails | POWER LEVEL: Medium

---

## 3. Curiosity Headlines (create intrigue — always pair with self-interest)
**"Do You Make These [number] Mistakes in [activity]?"**
Example: "Do You Make These Mistakes in English?"
BEST FOR: educational content, lead magnets, social, emails
POWER LEVEL: High — one of the most successful ads ever; "these" is the power word

**"What [authority/group] Do When [relatable situation]"**
Example: "What Everybody Ought to Know About This Stock and Bond Business"
BEST FOR: authority content, finance, health, professional topics
POWER LEVEL: Medium — works when the authority figure is relevant

**"Which of These [options] [do you want / is best]?"**
Example: "Which of These Five Skin Troubles Would You Like to End?"
BEST FOR: quizzes, comparison content, emails, landing pages
POWER LEVEL: Medium — readers scan for their own problem

**"Can You Pass This [topic] Test?"**
Example: "Can Your Kitchen Pass the Guest Test?"
BEST FOR: social, quizzes, interactive content, emails | POWER LEVEL: Medium

**"What's Wrong in [this picture / this situation]?"**
Example: "What's Wrong in This Picture?"
BEST FOR: social media, visual content, educational posts | POWER LEVEL: Medium

**"Why [surprising claim or counterintuitive fact]"**
Example: "Why Some People Almost Always Make Money in the Stock Market"
BEST FOR: blog posts, emails, long-form content, ads | POWER LEVEL: Medium

**"Do You Have These Symptoms of [problem]?"**
Example: "Do You Have These Symptoms of Nervous Exhaustion?"
BEST FOR: health, coaching, SaaS pain-point content
POWER LEVEL: Medium — multiple symptoms means almost every reader sees themselves

---

## 4. Quick/Easy Headlines (fast, effortless results — Caples' 4th quality)
**"[Result] in [short timeframe]"**
Example: "You Can Speak French by October 15"
BEST FOR: courses, coaching, SaaS, fitness, landing pages
POWER LEVEL: High — specific date/time beats vague "quickly"

**"The Lazy [person]'s Way to [benefit]"**
Example: "The Lazy Man's Way to Riches" (classic direct response ad)
BEST FOR: direct response, email subjects, social hooks
POWER LEVEL: High — widely cited formula; implies zero effort required

**"[Result] Without [dreaded activity]"**
Example: "Who Else Wants a Whiter Wash — With No Hard Work?"
BEST FOR: any offer where removing friction is the key sell
POWER LEVEL: High — eliminating pain is often stronger than promising gain

**"How I [got result] with [tiny investment]"**
Example: "How I Started a New Life with $7"
BEST FOR: case studies, testimonial ads, email subjects
POWER LEVEL: High — contrast between small input and big output drives curiosity

**"[Number] Minutes/Days to [desirable outcome]"**
Example: "Give Me 5 Days and I'll Give You a Magnetic Personality"
BEST FOR: courses, coaching, product promos, subject lines
POWER LEVEL: High — specificity makes the quick/easy claim believable

**"Only $[small amount] [gets you / buys you] [big value]"**
Example: "Only $2 a Week Buys This New Cassette Player"
BEST FOR: pricing-led ads, SaaS, subscription offers, retail
POWER LEVEL: Medium — breaking price into small units makes it feel easy

---

## 5. Command Headlines (direct instruction — tell the reader what to do)
**"Stop [painful activity / common mistake]"**
Example: "Stop Worrying About Money"
BEST FOR: social ads, email subjects, bold openers
POWER LEVEL: High — negative command stops the scroll; promises relief

**"Don't [buy/do X] Until You [read/see/try Y]"**
Example: "Don't Buy Car Insurance Until You Have Read These Facts"
BEST FOR: comparison content, buyer's guides, pre-launch
POWER LEVEL: High — "Don't buy" is an effective stopper because it's unexpected

**"[Imperative verb] Your Way to [benefit]"**
Example: "Grow Up. Not Old."
BEST FOR: social, brand campaigns, short-form | POWER LEVEL: Medium

**"Let Me [prove/show/tell] You [benefit]"**
Example: "Let Me Tell You How I Reduced for Keeps"
BEST FOR: direct response, emails, personal-brand content | POWER LEVEL: Medium

**"If [situation], [action]"**
Example: "If You Think You Can't Afford Quality Furniture, You Haven't Checked Our Leasing Terms"
BEST FOR: objection-handling ads, FAQ pages, retargeting | POWER LEVEL: Medium

**"Read This Before You [common action]"**
Example: "Read This Before You Order Your Zoysia Grass"
BEST FOR: buyer's guides, pre-purchase content, emails | POWER LEVEL: High

**"Because [reason], [command or benefit]"**
Example: "Because Fleas Come Back" (implied: use ongoing protection)
BEST FOR: reason-why ads, retargeting, subscriptions | POWER LEVEL: Medium

---

## Caples' Core Principles
1. **Self-interest beats everything.** Readers make time for what they want.
2. **Curiosity alone fails.** For every one that works, a dozen don't. Always pair with benefit.
3. **Specifics beat generalities.** "$35,840 in 2 years" beats "a lot of money."
4. **Long headlines that say something beat short ones that say nothing.**
5. **Test everything.** Caples saw one ad outsell another 19.5x.
6. **Believability matters.** Don't push "quick and easy" until it's unbelievable.
7. **Write 25 headlines, pick the best.** Ogilvy wrote 104 for the Rolls-Royce ad.
