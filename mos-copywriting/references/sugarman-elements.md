# Sugarman — 23 Copy Elements Checklist

Source: The Adweek Copywriting Handbook, Chapter 18 (Joseph Sugarman)

---

## Usage

Scan this checklist during editing. Not every element applies to every piece — but each one you miss is a potential conversion leak.

---

## The 23 Elements

| # | Element | What to Check | Priority |
|---|---------|--------------|----------|
| 1 | **Typeface** | Serif for body (readability), sans-serif for headlines. Match font to product personality. | Medium |
| 2 | **First Sentence** | Is it SHORT? Is it compelling enough to force reading the second sentence? | Critical |
| 3 | **Second Sentence** | Does it compel reading the third? The slide must be slippery from word one. | Critical |
| 4 | **Paragraph Headings** | Break up copy visually. Give scanners entry points. Make copy look less imposing. | High |
| 5 | **Product Explanation** | Complex products → explain simply. Simple products → add technical depth to increase perceived value. | High |
| 6 | **New Features** | Relate new features to things the reader already understands (linking). | Medium |
| 7 | **Technical Explanation** | Adds credibility and perceived value even if not fully understood. Gold-plated contacts, laser manufacturing. | Medium |
| 8 | **Anticipate Objections** | Raise objections yourself before the reader does. Ignoring an objection = ignoring the customer. | Critical |
| 9 | **Resolve Objections** | Every objection raised MUST be satisfactorily resolved. Installation, service, price — address them all. | Critical |
| 10 | **Gender** | Know your target audience. Avoid assumptions. Understand different priorities. | Low |
| 11 | **Clarity** | Keep copy clear, simple, short, to the point. Avoid big words. Exception: technical explanations for perceived value. | Critical |
| 12 | **Clichés** | Avoid them ruthlessly. "Here's the product the world has been waiting for" = lazy. If it sounds like ad copy, cut it. | High |
| 13 | **Rhythm** | Vary sentence length — short, long, medium, short, short, really long. Use triads for natural flow. | Medium |
| 14 | **Service** | For expensive/complex products, address service concerns explicitly. Modular parts, warranties, loaner programs. | Medium |
| 15 | **Physical Facts** | Mention ALL physical facts — weight, dimensions, size, limits, speed. One missing fact = one excuse not to buy. | High |
| 16 | **Trial Period** | Offer at least one month, preferably two. Longer trial = fewer returns + more confidence. | High |
| 17 | **Price Comparison** | Compare to similar products. Most expensive → superior features. Less expensive → better value. | High |
| 18 | **Testimonials** | Add credibility. Celebrity, man-on-the-street, or unsolicited customer testimonials. Must match product context. | High |
| 19 | **Price** | Great price → large and obvious. Expensive → underplay but don't hide. Put in coupon or bold in copy. | Medium |
| 20 | **Offer Summary** | Summarize what they get near the end. "So here's my offer..." — clear, complete, scannable. | Critical |
| 21 | **Avoid Saying Too Much** | The biggest mistake. Ask: "Is there a simpler way to say this?" You can often cut 50-80%. | Critical |
| 22 | **Ease of Ordering** | Clear CTA, multiple payment options, simple checkout. Reduce friction at the point of action. | Critical |
| 23 | **Ask for the Order** | Always, near the end. "I urge you to buy this at no obligation, today." Many copywriters forget this. | Critical |

---

## Quick Editing Pass

Run through these 7 critical elements on every piece of copy:

1. Is the first sentence SHORT and compelling? (#2)
2. Have I raised AND resolved every likely objection? (#8, #9)
3. Is every sentence clear and simple? (#11)
4. Have I eliminated clichés and unnecessary words? (#12, #21)
5. Is there a clear offer summary? (#20)
6. Is ordering/acting easy and obvious? (#22)
7. Did I ask for the order? (#23)
