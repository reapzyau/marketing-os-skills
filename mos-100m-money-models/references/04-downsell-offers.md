# Chapter 4 — Downsell Offers (Turn Nos Into Yeses)

## Hormozi's why
"What to offer when they say no." Any offer made after a no is a downsell — selling more people from the same leads. The golden rule: offer something **different for less**, but **never the same thing for less** (that's discounting — it destroys trust). "Everybody buys something."

## Framework — rules + the plays (pick ONE)

**Rules (all downsells):** they said no to THIS offer, not all offers; downsells are trades (give something, get something); personalize, don't pressure; never drop the price on the same thing.

**1. Payment Plan Downsells** *(changes HOW they pay)* — *"How much can you put down today?"* Same product, same total price, split up. "Too expensive" usually means "too much **up front**." Ladder: reward paying in full (bake interest in, then offer a prepay discount) → 3rd-party financing → half now / half later → temperature check (1–10, want 8+) → thirds → even spread → free trial. **Align charges to paydays** (recoups ~⅓ of declined payments). Churn by cadence: monthly 10.7% / quarterly 5% / annual 2% — start high, work down.

**2. Trial With Penalty** *(changes HOW they pay)* — *"Do X, Y, Z and you start free."* The inverse of Win Your Money Back: they try free as long as they meet the terms; they pay only if they DON'T. Get the card → get the commitment → explain required check-ins (= upsell windows) → explain fees AFTER the card. For recurring products/services. "Just call it a Free Trial."

**3. Feature Downsells** *(changes WHAT they get)* — *"Why don't we try this instead?"* Lower the price by changing what they get (lesser quantity / quality / fewer components) — NOT a discount. Remove features highest-to-lowest value so they "re-upsell themselves." (Removing the money-back guarantee as a feature tripled close rate 25%→75%.) Name combinations ("The Whale Package" … "The Minimum"); temperature-check after two downsells; alternate feature + payment-plan downsells; barter discounts for reviews/referrals.

## Diagnostic questions (ask the user)
**Q (AskUserQuestion):** "Which downsell play do you build first?" — Payment Plan / Trial With Penalty / Feature.
Plus:
- "When they say no, is it the total price or the up-front amount?"
- "Is your offer recurring (→ Trial With Penalty) or one-time?"
- "Which features do customers love vs tolerate (what to cut)?"
- "Do you have a guarantee you could remove as a feature downsell?"
- "Can you align payments to their paydays?"

## Validation criteria (what "good" looks like)
- Multiple offers for the SAME product (never the same thing cheaper)
- Payday-aligned plans / named feature tiers / reused Win-Your-Money-Back criteria for trials
- A 1–10 temperature check; alternating downsells

Push back if:
- Dropping the price on the same thing (the trust-killer)
- Pressuring; no temperature check; one rigid offer
- Putting paid-in-full buyers onto payment plans

## Pitfalls (Hormozi's warnings)
- Lowering the price on the SAME thing (the car-insurance "I didn't trust the guy" story)
- Negotiating with "business terrorists"; nickel-and-diming with fees instead of results
- Not aligning payments to paydays; putting paid-in-fulls on plans

## Workbook output expected
ONE downsell play that changes how-they-pay or what-they-get (never the same thing cheaper), with mechanics, a temperature check, and an optional barter-for-advertising option.
