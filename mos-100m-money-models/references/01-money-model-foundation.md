# Chapter 1 — What's a Money Model? (Foundation + the 30-Day Cash Math)

## Hormozi's why
A Money Model is "a sequence of offers" — every chance to solve a customer's problem, offered in order. The engine: make more profit from a customer than it costs to get + serve them, *fast* — ideally inside the first **30 days**. When you do, "the cost of getting more customers will never be a problem again" and growth self-funds (Client-Financed Acquisition).

## Framework
- **Definition:** a Money Model = a deliberate sequence of offers (what you offer, when, how). The rental-car story: a $19/day car became $100/day via 5 sequenced offers (upgrade → late return → premium insurance → minimum-insurance downsell → prepaid gas) = 5×.
- **The engine — Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA):** make 30-day gross profit per customer ≥ cost to get + serve them. "I like to cover my cost of getting a customer within 30 days" — because any business can get ~30 days interest-free on a credit card. Get a customer on credit, pay it back in 30 days, repeat. A **$100M Money Model** makes more from ONE customer in 30 days than it costs to get + serve *several* — removing cash as a scaling limiter.
- **Bad money models kill businesses:** when CAC > profit, you cut ads, float on loans, sell equity, lose it. Two options: wait ~2 years to recoup (only works if you already have cash), or get paid fast and grow freely.
- **The math of winning:** double customer value × double volume × double speed ≈ 8× faster growth; triple each ≈ 27×.
- **The four offer types (sequenced in this order):** Attraction (strangers → customers), Upsell (spend more), Downsell (turn nos → yeses), Continuity (keep buying).

## Diagnostic questions (ask the user)
Mostly open + numbers:
1. "How much cash do you have, and how many months of runway is that?"
2. "What's your cost to acquire a customer (CAC) vs your 30-day gross profit per customer?"
3. "How many offers do you currently make a customer, and in what order?"
4. "Which of the four offer types do you already have, and which are missing?"
5. "Can you recoup acquisition cost inside 30 days today — yes or no?"

The chapter's output: the 30-day CFA target + an honest payback-gap diagnosis.

## Validation criteria (what "good" looks like)
- A real number for CAC and 30-day gross profit per customer (or a commitment to measure it)
- A clear statement of the payback gap (how far from 30-day breakeven on acquisition)
- The user can name which offer types they have vs need

Push back if:
- "I just sell one thing" with no idea of CAC vs payback — that's the problem the model fixes
- Vibes instead of numbers — force the CAC + 30-day GP figures
- They want to skip to tactics before knowing the gap

## Pitfalls (Hormozi's warnings)
- Relying on the slow drip of profit across many customers (starves cash)
- Only being able to scale if you already have lots of cash
- Copying "what they do" instead of designing your own model
- Hard-selling weak products; ignoring the law (esp. "free"); not being transparent

## Workbook output expected
