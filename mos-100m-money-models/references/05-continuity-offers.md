# Chapter 5 — Continuity Offers (Keep Them Buying) — sequence LAST

## Hormozi's why
"You can shear a sheep for a lifetime, but you can only skin it once." Continuity = ongoing value for ongoing payments until cancel — "sell once, get paid again and again." But on its own it crashes 30-day cash, so it goes **last**: attraction/upsell/downsell first, then continuity. Core math: a $1,000 one-off → 10 buyers = $10k now; the same at $50/mo → 40 buyers × 20 months = $2k now + $40k later (and 4× the customers to upsell).

## Framework — the plays (pick ONE)

**1. Continuity Bonus Offers** — *"If you like this, you'll love what's next."* Give an awesome bonus (worth more than month one) for signing up today; advertise the bonus, not the membership. Keep it related to the core offer; use things you already have; give titles (silver/gold/diamond); anchor the value realistically. **Pricing-for-continuity:** to get X% onto continuity, price the standalone at a multiple — 50%→1.33×, 60%→1.66×, 70%→2×, 80%→2.33×, 90%→2.66×.

**2. Continuity Discount Offers** — *"Sign up today, get X time free."* Free time for committing to pay over time (continuity version of Buy-X-Get-Y). Discount four ways: up front (push out the term; needs enforceable contracts) / at the end (earn free time by paying on time) / spread over time / after the first 1–2 payments (collect cash + validate the card first). **Highest-value tip in the book: bill every 4 weeks, not monthly** → 13 cycles/yr = **+8.3% revenue (+41% profit at 20% margins)** for no extra work. Add a 3% processing fee; get 2 payment forms + prefer ACH; lifetime discount earned at your worst churn month; cancellation = repay the discount received; always do exit interviews (~⅓ saved).

**3. Waived Fee Offer** — *"Month-to-month with a setup fee, or I'll waive it for a 12-month commitment."* Charge a startup fee (typically **3–5× monthly**) on month-to-month; waive it for a 1-year+ commitment; early cancel = pay the fee. Fees get them to **start** (avoid the fee) and to **stick** (cost to quit > cost to stay). Best for 1-year+ commitments and slow-to-work services (SEO, weight loss, investing).

## Diagnostic questions (ask the user)
**Q (AskUserQuestion):** "Which continuity play fits — reward-based or contract-based?" — Continuity Bonus / Continuity Discount / Waived Fee.
Plus:
- "Does your product deliver ongoing value (justifying ongoing pay)?"
- "What % of customers do you want on continuity (sets the standalone multiple)?"
- "Could you bill every 4 weeks instead of monthly?"
- "When do customers typically churn (sets the lifetime-discount threshold)?"
- "Do you have a clear, easy cancellation + exit-interview path?"

## Validation criteria (what "good" looks like)
- Continuity placed LAST in the sequence (not used as the attraction offer)
- A high-value related bonus / 4-week billing / a churn-point lifetime discount / clear cancel terms
- The standalone-price multiple matches the target continuity %

Push back if:
- Continuity used as a standalone attraction offer (cash-starved)
- A bare membership with nothing to upsell ("no successful continuity business has a standalone membership offer")
- Monthly billing left on the table; no exit interview; harsh fees that breed resentment

## Pitfalls (Hormozi's warnings)
- Using continuity as a standalone attraction offer (crashes 30-day cash)
- A standalone membership with no upsell bells/whistles
- Leaving monthly billing on the table; no exit interview; fees so harsh they handcuff people into resentment (look into it if >5% cancel early)
