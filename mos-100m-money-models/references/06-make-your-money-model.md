# Chapter 6 — Make Your Money Model (Assemble + the Cash Math)

## Hormozi's why
"How to take over your entire market." Combine the four offer types into a deliberate sequence so you make enough from one customer to get + serve **at least two more in under 30 days** — which "eliminates cash as a bottleneck for growth."

## Framework
- **Definition:** a Money Model = a deliberate sequence of offers — what you offer, when, how — to make as much money as fast as possible.
- **Three stages:** Stage I **Get Cash** (Attraction — more customers for less) → Stage II **Get More Cash** (Upsell & Downsell — more money, faster) → Stage III **Get The Most Cash** (Continuity — maximize lifetime spend).
- **The cash math (tiers):** (1) a good model = 30-day profit from a customer ≥ cost to get + serve them; (2) a **$100M model** = 30-day profit from ONE customer ≥ cost to get + serve *several* (Client-Financed Acquisition).
- **Order of operations:** "I get customers reliably → they pay for themselves → they pay for *other* customers → I maximize each customer's lifetime value → I spend as much on ads as I can to print money." Each stage funds the next.
- **Build one stage at a time.** A full model assembled from zero "will collapse on top of you." Make ONE offer reliable, then add the next.
- **4-step assembly:** Attraction (cover cost) → Upsell (30-day profit above cost) → Downsell (turn nos into yeses) → Continuity (one last sale + recurring). (Gym Launch evolution: Decoy → $476k/mo → +upsell/continuity/downsell → $1.5M → +menu/feature → $2.3M → +Prestige Labs → $4.4M/mo.) "Simple Scales, Fancy Fails."

## Diagnostic questions (ask the user)
1. "Which stage are you in — getting customers, paying for themselves, or maximizing LTV?"
2. "Sequence your chosen plays: attraction → upsell → downsell → continuity — does the order fit your buyer's journey?"
3. "Does the sequence recoup CAC + COGS inside 30 days? Run the numbers."
4. "Which ONE play do you implement FIRST, and what triggers adding the next?"
5. "Any gaps you could fill with affiliate products instead of building new ones?"
6. "What price do you start at to guarantee lots of early yeses?"

## Validation criteria (what "good" looks like)
- A named offer at each of the four stages, sequenced in order
- 30-day cash math showing one customer funds ≥2 more
- A build order: ONE play first, with a trigger to add the next (built one stage at a time)

Push back if:
- Trying to deploy the full model at once ("it will break your business")
- More products instead of more offers ("Simple Scales, Fancy Fails")
- No payback math; continuity placed too early; measuring in weeks not quarters

## Pitfalls (Hormozi's warnings)
- Implementing a whole money model at once (breaks the business)
- Starting more businesses just to make more offers
- Measuring in weeks not quarters ("build it right or build it again")
- Forcing continuity at the wrong time; failing to get customers at a profit (run out of cash)

## Workbook output expected
The assembled money model: a named play at each stage, sequenced; the 30-day cash math proving CFA (one customer funds ≥2 more); and a build order that implements one stage at a time. This completes the trilogy.
