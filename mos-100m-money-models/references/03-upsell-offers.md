# Chapter 3 — Upsell Offers (Get Customers To Spend More)

## Hormozi's why
"Do you want fries with that?" With customers in hand, upsells maximize 30-day profit — and "often, upsells make the majority of the profit." Every solved problem reveals the next; the upsell solves it, best made right after a yes (current customers buy at far higher rates than strangers).

## Framework — the plays (pick ONE+ to start)

**1. The Classic Upsell** — *"You can't have X without Y."* Solve the NEXT problem your core offer creates, the moment they feel it (car wash → sealant; bike → helmet; course → next course). Offer higher-profit upsells first; sell into the **hyper-buying cycle**; bundle + name packages; **BAMFAM (Book A Meeting From A Meeting)**; charge for guarantees/warranties. (Burger: $0.25 → $3.00 profit, 11.6×.)

**2. Menu Upsell** — *"You don't need that, you need this."* Up to 4 tactics: **Unselling** (what they DON'T need), **Prescription** (exactly what they DO need + how to use it), **A/B Upsell** (ask preference A or B, not yes/no), **Card On File** ("just use the card on file?"). The Economist decoy: add a 3rd option priced = the bundle so most take the combo.

**3. Anchor Upsell** — present a premium thing first at **5–10× the price** (get "the gasp"), then the main offer feels like relief. Main + premium share the same PRIMARY features, differ only on secondary ones. Make a premium you'd actually be happy to sell (some buy it = outsized profit). *"The only thing worse than making a $1,000 offer to someone with a $100 budget is making a $100 offer to someone with a $1,000 budget."*

**4. Rollover Upsell** — *"Wanna roll it forward?"* Credit some/all of a prior purchase toward your next (bigger) offer. Next offer ≥ **4× the rollover credit** (≤25% discount). Add one-time urgency. (Winback: 200 personalized videos, $4k credit each, 20% took it → ~$1.9M/yr.)

## Diagnostic questions (ask the user)
**Q (AskUserQuestion):** "Which upsell play do you start with?" — Classic / Menu / Anchor / Rollover.
Plus:
- "What's the immediate next problem your core offer creates (→ Classic)?"
- "Do you have multiple products solving the same problem (→ Menu/A/B)?"
- "Do you have a premium version you'd genuinely want sold (→ Anchor)?"
- "Past/upset/competitor customers + credit to roll over (→ Rollover)?"
- "Which upsell has the highest margin to offer first?"

## Validation criteria (what "good" looks like)
- A specific next-problem upsell (not "more of the same")
- Named bundles; anchor priced 5–10× with shared primary features; rollover priced ≥4× credit
- A card-on-file / BAMFAM step

Push back if:
- Single front-end product, no real next offer ("you barely have a business — you have a front end")
- Fake anchor; discounting instead of upselling
- Not asking at all ("the worst thing is if they'd have said yes but you never asked")

## Pitfalls (Hormozi's warnings)
- Offering the wrong thing / wrong time / wrong way; only selling one thing
- Treating the anchor as fake; rolling over so much credit there's no profit left
- Not asking
